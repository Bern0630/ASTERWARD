## Investment Thesis

Asia contains the world's densest concentration of foundries, packaging, memory, servers, networking, power equipment and data-center investment. Order growth does not automatically produce sustainable shareholder returns. The Federal Reserve lifted its policy range to 3.75%-4.00%, the Bank of Japan raised its rate to 1.25%, and the US ten-year Treasury yield briefly exceeded 5% this week. With the risk-free rate, energy cost and currency volatility rising together, research must progress from who receives orders to who pays, who advances capital, who borrows and when assets begin producing cash.

TSMC's August revenue grew 53.3% from a year earlier. Taiwan's TAIEX and OTC market rose together on September 18. Malaysia's August exports increased 45.5% and imports 41.1%. These data show that Asian technology and capital-goods activity remains strong, but they do not establish returns on capital. **The purpose of this deep dive is not to deny AI growth. It is to separate one unit of growth into revenue, working capital, capital expenditure, debt and ultimate cash recovery.**

## Supply-Chain Capital Map

AI infrastructure has five layers. The first is chip design, foundries, memory and advanced packaging. The second is servers, racks, cooling, power supplies, printed circuit boards and optical networking. The third is data-center buildings, land, substations, cooling and connectivity. The fourth is generation, transmission, natural gas, renewable power and storage. Only the fifth layer contains the cloud platforms, model companies, enterprises and governments generating the final workloads.

Each layer can record the prior layer's capital expenditure as its own revenue. A cloud company buys servers; a server company buys chips and components; a data center applies for power; and a utility builds grid capacity. Adding every announced order and capital budget double counts the same end demand. The correct approach follows cash to the final payer and asks whether each intermediate layer is earning a return or merely passing costs downstream.

## Who Pays

Final payers include hyperscale cloud providers, AI laboratories, enterprises, governments and consumers. Payment quality depends on the contracting entity, minimum purchases, prepayments, take-or-pay clauses, parent guarantees and termination rights. A famous brand in a project announcement does not prove that the parent company guarantees every payment. The tenant may be a subsidiary, reseller or special-purpose vehicle.

High-quality demand appears in cloud revenue, AI-service usage, enterprise renewal and minimum-payment obligations. Lower-quality demand depends on the next fundraising round, supplier credit or distant utilization. A model company buys compute from a cloud platform, the platform buys servers, and the server company orders chips. The same expected revenue can be described as demand at three levels, but only final-customer cash closes the loop.

## Who Advances Capital

Foundries and packaging companies must build facilities and acquire equipment before orders become revenue. Server and component companies hold inventory, extend receivables and pay suppliers. Data-center developers pay for land, construction, interconnection and interest before rent begins. Utilities may build substations and generation years before actual load arrives.

Advancing capital is not inherently negative, but pricing, contracts and capital structure must compensate for it. Revenue growth cannot be analyzed alone. If receivables and inventory repeatedly grow faster than revenue, the cash-conversion cycle lengthens and suppliers finance more of their customers' growth. Rising prepayments and contract liabilities show that customers share more construction risk.

## Who Borrows

Borrowers include manufacturers, electronics assemblers, data-center developers, real-estate investment trusts, utilities, telecom companies, infrastructure funds and special-purpose vehicles. Debt can be corporate bonds, bank loans, project finance, equipment leases, vendor finance, securitization or private credit. A brand company with net cash may still place leverage in purchase commitments, leases and partners elsewhere in the chain.

Asset life must match debt maturity. Foundries and grids can operate for more than a decade, while GPUs, networking equipment and some cooling systems have shorter economic lives. Financing rapidly depreciating equipment with short-term floating-rate debt under a long-duration real-estate valuation exposes borrowers to interest-rate, residual-value and refinancing risk simultaneously.

## Taiwan: Manufacturing and Working Capital

Taiwan's advantage is its integrated manufacturing cluster, advanced processes, packaging, servers and component execution. TSMC's 53.3% year-over-year August revenue growth is the clearest demand anchor. Friday's simultaneous gains in TSMC, MediaTek, ASE and the OTC market also show that capital did not rely only on one foundry.

Taiwan's capital risk is less about data-center real estate and more about expansion, inventory, receivables, customer concentration and thin-margin assembly. Rapid server shipments can produce strong revenue growth while expensive chips, memory and components raise inventory values. Longer payment terms or delayed customer acceptance increase short-term funding needs. Revenue, operating cash flow, inventory, receivables and short-term borrowing must be compared together.

Foreign cash buying of NT$86.994 billion improved equity liquidity, but foreign investors still held a 76,110-contract index-futures net short. Capital markets continued to distinguish stock fundamentals from macro risk. A higher share price can lower equity-financing cost without directly reducing dollar borrowing cost, lease expense or customer-credit risk.

## Japan: Funding Currency and Equipment Supply

Japan is both a source of global funding currency and a supplier of semiconductor equipment and materials. The yen weakened toward 158 after the BOJ raised rates to 1.25%, so there was no immediate carry unwind. The policy rate has nevertheless entered the lower end of the BOJ's estimated 1.1%-2.5% neutral range. Faster future hikes or a rapid yen appreciation could increase funding costs and collateral requirements for leveraged capital globally.

Japanese equipment and materials companies may benefit from the weak yen and Asian fab expansion, but remain exposed to the capital-expenditure cycle of their customers. Backlogs must be separated into cancellable and non-cancellable orders, prepayment, delivery schedule and margin. If higher yields cause customers to delay fabs, equipment revenue will usually reflect the problem after orders begin weakening.

## Malaysia: Power and Project Finance

Malaysia represents the data-center and power layers. MIDA reported RM385.7 billion of data-center-related investment from 2021 through the first half of 2026 and cited roughly 4.6 GW planned or under construction. Separately, 49 power-supply agreements represent maximum demand of 7.1 GW expected to be used over five to ten years. Confirmed actual load was roughly 710 MW in September 2025. The figures describe different stages and should not be added or substituted for one another.

Malaysia's August total trade rose 43.4% from a year earlier, with exports up 45.5%, imports up 41.1% and a RM28.1 billion surplus. Inflation increased from 1.8% to 1.9%, led by transport inflation rising from 1.4% to 2.0%. Electronics and capital-goods activity is strong while energy and construction costs are rising. A data center must secure land, power, finance, pre-leasing, energization and utilization. Any delay raises construction interest and postpones rent commencement.

Key project-finance metrics include loan-to-value, debt-service coverage, interest coverage, fixed versus floating rates, completion guarantees, tenant minimum payments and debt maturity. A power agreement reduces interconnection uncertainty but does not mean generation, transmission, finance and tenants are all in place.

## Cash Conversion

The supply chain must pass three cash-conversion tests. First, revenue must be collected rather than remain in receivables. Second, capital expenditure must generate incremental operating profit above the weighted average cost of capital. Third, free cash flow must cover interest, maintain the asset and return capital to shareholders.

Manufacturers can be assessed through operating cash flow relative to earnings, free-cash-flow margin and the cash-conversion cycle. Data centers require energized capacity, pre-leasing, rent commencement, utilization, unit rent, net operating income and debt-service coverage. Utilities require regulated recovery, rate base, debt maturity and pass-through of power costs.

## Credit Transmission

Credit risk does not need to begin with canceled orders. The more common sequence is delayed equipment delivery or grid connection, delayed customer acceptance, rising receivables, operating cash flow below earnings, higher short-term borrowing and lower interest coverage. Capital expenditure cuts or canceled orders appear later. Equity may still reward revenue growth while credit markets begin demanding a higher spread.

Valuation creates another transmission channel. A higher risk-free rate raises capitalization rates, lowers data-center asset values and mechanically increases loan-to-value ratios. A borrower may need to add collateral or equity even if operating income is unchanged. Losses for special-purpose vehicles and private lenders become larger if equipment residual values fall at the same time.

## Bull Case

The constructive case requires continued end usage and cloud revenue, delivery by Taiwan manufacturers without a material extension in the cash-conversion cycle, an orderly yen and a steady conversion of Malaysian data-center plans into financed, pre-leased and energized capacity. Lower oil keeps the US ten-year yield below 5% and credit spreads remain contained.

Beneficiaries would begin with foundries, advanced packaging and platforms that have the clearest cash flow, followed by power, cooling, networking and equipment suppliers with pricing power, and only later by highly leveraged projects without energized capacity or diversified tenants. Equity gains accompanied by better operating cash flow and interest coverage would represent high-quality growth.

## Bear Case

The adverse case is renewed oil inflation, faster global central-bank tightening and simultaneous pressure on dollar and yen funding. Companies do not need to cancel their AI strategies. Delaying lower-priority projects, reducing prepayments or demanding longer supplier terms would be enough to shift financing pressure into Asia's supply chain.

A second adverse path is end usage growing more slowly than construction. Chips, servers, data centers and grids all expand on the same high-growth assumption, but model efficiency, customer consolidation or power bottlenecks leave utilization below underwriting. Revenue may keep growing while return on capital, asset turnover and free cash flow deteriorate first.

## Most Fragile Links

The most fragile links are special-purpose vehicles using short-term floating-rate funding to buy rapidly depreciating equipment without long contracts or parent guarantees. Next are fast-growing, low-margin suppliers with rapidly rising receivables and inventory. The third group is data-center projects that commit capital to grids and land before obtaining high-quality pre-leasing.

Large chip and platform companies still have risk, but they possess larger cash and pricing buffers. Material leverage may sit outside public financial statements in leases, purchase commitments, partners, resellers and project companies. Those obligations must be recombined to see the leverage of the full chain.

## Leading Indicators

1. Cloud AI revenue, paid workloads and utilization, not capital expenditure alone.
2. The joint direction of Taiwan supplier revenue, margin, inventory, receivables and operating cash flow.
3. Equipment-order prepayments, cancellation rights, delivery schedules and customer concentration.
4. The BOJ path, USD/JPY and volatility in yen-funded positions.
5. Malaysian megawatts that are planned, power-secured, financed, pre-leased, energized and utilized.
6. Data-center loan-to-value, debt-service coverage, interest coverage and refinancing spreads.
7. US two-year and ten-year yields, oil, the dollar and credit spreads.
8. Foreign Taiwan cash flows, index-futures hedges, domestic margin and OTC breadth.

## Falsification

If global yields decline and credit spreads narrow without a demand recession, while AI usage, revenue, cash flow and returns on capital rise together, capital-structure risk would become secondary and supply scarcity would again dominate. Conversely, if end revenue falls broadly enough that cloud and model leaders also cut capital expenditure, the thesis would shift from capital-structure differentiation to a downcycle in demand.

## Next Confirmation

US August industrial production was unchanged, manufacturing output fell 0.3%, and capacity utilization held at 76.3%. The near-term test is whether US yields remain inside this week's range after the softer report and whether Taiwan foreign cash buying continues while futures shorts decline. The medium-term test is third-quarter operating cash flow, inventory, receivables, capital expenditure and interest expense.

Malaysia needs MIDA, regulators, utilities and developers to separate power-secured, financed, pre-leased, energized and utilized capacity. Japan requires monitoring wages, underlying inflation and the pace of BOJ tightening. Order growth becomes durable cash flow only when end usage, supply execution and financing conditions improve together.
