## Research Thesis

September 18 produced a cross-market combination that looks contradictory but is internally coherent. The Bank of Japan lifted its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25%, yet the yen fell to a two-week low. Foreign investors bought NT$86.994 billion of Taiwan cash equities while retaining a 76,110-contract net short in Taiwan index futures. The TAIEX closed at its high and the OTC index gained 3.64%. Any one observation can mislead. Together they show that capital is willing to add Taiwan AI and semiconductor exposure without abandoning protection against global interest-rate, energy and currency risk.

**This is not simply foreign investors turning bullish on Taiwan or bearish through futures. It is a structure that seeks single-stock excess return in cash equities while controlling macro beta with index futures.** A weaker yen prevented an immediate forced carry unwind and gave Asian risk assets room. A Japanese policy rate at the lower end of the estimated neutral range preserves the future deleveraging risk.

## Evidence and Market Pricing

The evidence has three parts: the yen weakened after a BOJ rate increase, foreign investors bought Taiwan cash equities aggressively, and the large index-futures net short declined only modestly. Markets therefore increased exposure to Taiwan stock selection while retaining macro protection, pricing strong relative returns in Asian technology without declaring that global funding risk had disappeared.

## Initial Signal: The Yen Weakens After a Hike

The BOJ voted 7-2 to raise the policy rate to a 31-year high. Governor Kazuo Ueda said the policy phase had shifted from lifting underlying inflation toward 2% to preventing an overshoot. He did not rule out consecutive increases or a 50-basis-point move. In terms of the medium-term reaction function, this was not a dovish hike.

Two dissents nevertheless caused markets to question the speed of later moves. The dollar rose more than 1.2% against the yen to roughly 158.07, while Japan's ten-year government-bond yield declined. Prices traded a future path below the most hawkish expectation, not the disappearance of Japan's inflation problem. The Nikkei 225 gained about 1.4%, showing that the funding currency did not force same-day risk-asset deleveraging.

## Second Signal: A Large Taiwan Cash Re-Entry

Taiwan gained 892.75 points, or 1.93%, to 47,180.75 on Friday with NT$1.075 trillion of listed-market turnover. The OTC index rose 3.64% to 412.68. There were 759 listed advancers and 258 decliners, extending the rebound from TSMC, MediaTek and ASE to smaller companies. Price, volume and breadth provide more convincing evidence of cash demand than a gap higher in one index heavyweight.

The three major institutional groups bought a combined NT$116.587 billion, led by NT$86.994 billion from foreign investors, up sharply from NT$12.185 billion one session earlier. After selling NT$17.980 billion on September 16, foreign investors bought for two consecutive sessions. Their willingness to add Taiwan cash equities after the Fed and BOJ decisions indicates lower policy uncertainty and continuing confidence in the relative earnings of the AI supply chain.

## Third Signal: Futures Protection Remains

Foreign investors held 10,323 long and 86,433 short open index-futures contracts. Their net short declined from 78,674 to 76,110 contracts. Some shorts were covered, but the change was small relative to the jump in cash buying. The options volume put/call ratio was 99.35% and the open-interest ratio 76.32%, so the options market did not build a stronger stock of downside protection at the same time.

The more reasonable combined reading is that foreign institutions increased Taiwan technology and stock-specific exposure through cash while retaining global-risk protection through index futures. Published investor categories aggregate many institutions, so the cash and futures legs cannot be treated as a precise trade by one account. The aggregate divergence can still show that confidence in single-stock alpha exceeds confidence in global beta.

## Transmission Mechanism

The BOJ hiked with a divided vote, the yen weakened, and yen-funded positions avoided a forced unwind. Asian risk assets received short-term liquidity room, foreign capital bought Taiwan semiconductor and AI cash equities, and Taiwan turnover, breadth and the OTC market strengthened together. Global rates and energy risk remained elevated, so foreign investors retained a large index-futures net short as macro protection.

Every link can break. A weaker yen does not guarantee permanently easy Asian liquidity. Foreign cash buying does not guarantee that futures shorts decline. A higher TAIEX does not prevent the US ten-year yield from moving above 5%. A strong signal requires these markets to confirm one another over several sessions.

## Implications for Taiwan Technology Valuation

A weaker yen has two effects on Taiwan technology. In the short run, it avoids a forced unwind of yen-funded positions and reduces liquidity pressure on high-multiple Asian equities. Japanese electronics and equipment exporters may benefit from the exchange rate, supporting regional technology sentiment. Over time, a weak yen raises imported inflation, potentially forcing faster BOJ tightening while increasing price competition for Taiwanese companies in Japan.

If the dollar strengthens as well, foreign investors must consider the currency return on Taiwan assets. A weaker Taiwan dollar can offset part of the equity gain. Foreign institutions can therefore like selected shares and still retain index or currency hedges. Cash buying and a futures net short are not logically inconsistent.

## Bull Case

The cleanest bullish signal would be a stable USD/JPY rather than a sharp move in either direction, a US ten-year yield below 5%, consecutive foreign cash buying, a futures net short below 70,000 contracts, and continued strength in OTC breadth and advancing issues. That would indicate orderly carry positions, stable dollar funding and an expansion from stock selection into broader risk acceptance.

The signal improves further if TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and smaller Taiwan electronics companies rise together. US technology earnings, Taiwan supply execution and Asian capital flows would then create a reinforcing cycle.

## Bear Case

The most dangerous combination is not one BOJ hike. It is a rapid yen appreciation together with rising US yields, tighter dollar funding and larger Taiwan index-futures shorts. That would pressure carry leverage, equity discount rates and foreign-exchange returns simultaneously. If a few index heavyweights still hold the TAIEX up while the OTC market and breadth weaken, headline prices may delay recognition of the risk.

Another adverse signal would be the sudden disappearance of foreign cash buying while turnover and domestic margin financing continue to rise. Institutional re-entry would have ended and leveraged chasing would have taken over. If futures protection remained high, liquidity could deteriorate quickly during a reversal.

## Leading Indicators

1. Whether USD/JPY stabilizes instead of extending a large one-way move.
2. Whether Japanese two-year and ten-year yields rise again as later BOJ moves are priced.
3. Three-day and five-day cumulative foreign Taiwan cash flows rather than one session.
4. Whether the foreign index-futures net short continues to decline from 76,110 contracts.
5. Whether the index-options open-interest put/call ratio rises while equities advance.
6. Joint confirmation from the TAIEX, OTC index, advancing issues and turnover.
7. Whether US two-year and ten-year yields and the dollar break above this week's highs together.

## Falsification

If foreign cash buying persists and the futures net short quickly returns to a neutral range, the stock-selection-plus-macro-hedge framework would be invalidated in favor of a direct broad risk-on regime. Conversely, simultaneous cash selling, larger futures shorts, OTC weakness and a sharp yen rally would also invalidate it because capital would have moved from retaining protection to actively reducing risk.

## Next Confirmation

The first test is the Friday US close for TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and Treasury yields. The second is whether foreign investors continue buying Taiwan cash equities next session and whether the 76,110-contract net short materially declines. The third is Japan's next inflation and wage data and BOJ communication, which will show whether markets reprice the speed of hikes.

Only confirmation across currencies, rates, cash equities, futures and breadth would turn Friday's move into a durable cross-market flow signal. Until then, it is better described as fundamental stock selection coexisting with macro protection.
