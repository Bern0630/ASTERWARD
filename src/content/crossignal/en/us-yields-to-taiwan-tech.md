## Initial Signal

The cross-market signal begins with the US risk-free rate. On September 15, two-, ten- and thirty-year Treasury yields were approximately 4.671%, 5.002% and 5.367%, respectively, while the ten-year reached 5.041% intraday. During Asian trading on September 16, the ten-year eased to approximately 4.9875% but remained high enough to alter equity discount rates.

This signal has two distinct implications for Taiwan technology. The first is a valuation shock: higher long yields raise the cost of equity and reduce the multiple investors will pay. The second is a fundamental shock: restrictive rates eventually reduce consumption, cloud capex and corporate investment, which then reaches chip, server and component orders. The two occur on different timelines. An early share-price decline should not automatically be classified as weaker earnings.

## Transmission Chain

```text
Energy and inflation risk rises
↓
Expected Federal Reserve path moves higher
↓
US two- and ten-year yields rise
↓
The dollar firms and global equity discount rates increase
↓
Foreign investors reduce Asian exposure or add futures hedges
↓
Taiwan large-cap technology multiples compress
↓
High-valuation, distant-earnings and capital-intensive suppliers become more volatile
```

Every link requires separate confirmation. Higher US yields need not strengthen the dollar. A stronger dollar need not produce foreign cash-market selling. Foreign selling does not by itself establish a semiconductor-order reversal. The strongest signal is alignment across rates, FX, cash equities, futures and market breadth over several sessions.

## Two-Session Evidence

On September 16, the Taiex gained 0.74% to 45,848.90, but foreign investors sold approximately NT$17.98 billion of cash equities and the three major institutional categories sold a combined NT$20.31 billion. Foreign investors sold approximately 8.74 million TSMC shares, 9.69 million Wistron shares, 2.77 million Wiwynn shares and 1.69 million Hon Hai shares. They bought approximately 2.61 million ASE Technology shares and 1.00 million MediaTek shares. The index gain was therefore not a broad foreign chase; dividend-gap recovery, domestic absorption and rotation also mattered.

On September 17, the Taiex added 0.96% to 46,288.00. Foreign cash buying turned positive at approximately NT$12.19 billion, investment trusts bought NT$7.71 billion and dealers' proprietary books bought NT$5.89 billion. After a NT$4.32 billion sale from dealer hedging books, the three categories bought a combined NT$21.48 billion. Foreign buying reached approximately 8.83-8.88 million shares each in Wistron and Hon Hai, 4.79 million in Quanta, 4.79 million in TSMC and 2.46 million in ASE Technology. Cash demand broadened from one large-cap name into servers and packaging, improving the quality of the rebound.

Futures did not confirm a clean risk-on shift. Foreign net open interest in Taiwan index futures moved from approximately 76,351 contracts net short on September 16 to 78,674 net short on September 17. September 16 was monthly settlement, so contract rollover distorts a one-day comparison. The position should not be treated as a direct forecast. Still, heavier futures hedging alongside cash buying suggests that foreign investors were willing to add Taiwan equities without removing macro protection.

The Taiwan index-options volume Put/Call ratio rose from 106.36% to 107.24%, while the open-interest ratio fell from 83.56% to 76.50%. Trading flow retained some put demand, but outstanding positions did not show broad accumulation of downside protection. Options combine hedging, income and arbitrage strategies, so this indicator should be read with futures and cash flows rather than alone.

## Fundamentals Versus Valuation

TSMC's August revenue reached NT$514.806 billion, up 10.1% month on month and 53.3% year on year, providing a firm fundamental anchor. As long as TSMC, AI server, advanced-packaging and high-speed interconnect revenue and orders do not weaken together, higher rates initially affect valuation rather than current chip demand.

Sensitivity differs across the supply chain. TSMC and large systems companies have stronger cash flow and customer visibility, allowing earnings growth to offset part of the discount-rate increase. Suppliers expanding capacity while receivables and inventory rise, or those with concentrated orders, face both multiple and working-capital pressure. Delta Electronics, thermal, PCB and power-equipment companies must also be judged on the distinction between structural data-center demand and expectations already embedded in high valuations.

## Positioning Framework

The first layer is aggregate cash flow: whether foreign investors, investment trusts and dealers move together. The second is breadth across large caps and suppliers: whether buying is limited to TSMC or reaches Hon Hai, Quanta, Wistron, Wiwynn, ASE Technology, MediaTek, Delta, PCB and thermal suppliers. The third is futures: whether foreign investors add Taiwan index hedges. The fourth is options and margin: whether professional protection costs rise while retail leverage also expands.

On September 16, listed-market margin financing rose from NT$582.240 billion to NT$586.167 billion, an increase of approximately NT$3.93 billion, while foreign investors sold cash equities and the index advanced. Domestic and leveraged demand was strong enough to absorb the selling. If foreign investors sell again while futures shorts and margin balances continue to rise, positioning would shift from support to vulnerability.

## Confirmation and Invalidation

Bullish confirmation requires three conditions: continued revenue growth at TSMC and across the AI supply chain, foreign cash buying that broadens beyond one large cap, and a US ten-year yield that does not hold above 5.05%. A reduction in foreign futures shorts would strengthen the signal.

Bearish confirmation would be a simultaneous rise in US long yields, the dollar and oil, accompanied by renewed foreign cash selling, larger Taiwan futures shorts, and weaker OTC and smaller suppliers relative to the index. Only a concurrent decline in monthly revenue or cloud capex guidance would move the problem from valuation into fundamentals.

The signal is invalidated if yields stay high while TSMC and supply-chain earnings estimates continue to rise, foreign investors remain cash buyers, the Taiwan dollar is stable and breadth improves. In that regime, earnings growth is absorbing the higher discount rate, and the rule that higher Treasury yields must depress Taiwan technology becomes unreliable.

## Next Confirmation

Track the US ten-year yield around 5.00%-5.05%, USD/TWD, aggregate foreign cash flow, foreign net Taiwan-futures open interest, the Taiwan index-options Put/Call ratio, and monthly revenue from TSMC and server suppliers. The valuable signal is not one day's net flow. It is whether price, cash, futures and fundamentals remain aligned for three to five sessions.
