**Primary thesis:** Index trends in the United States and Taiwan remained constructive, but leadership concentration was increasing. Semiconductors and a record Nasdaq supported the US, while foreign cash buying supported Taiwan. Both markets needed better breadth to confirm that high-level momentum reflected broad risk appetite rather than a small group of large stocks.

**Secondary signal 1:** US long yields stayed near 5%, while oil fell early before trimming its decline. Discount-rate and inflation pressure did not worsen, but neither created a fresh easing impulse.

**Secondary signal 2:** Foreign investors bought Taiwan cash equities aggressively while adding index-futures shorts. Margin-financing value rose as financed shares declined, indicating that capital was concentrating in higher-priced leaders while index hedging remained in place.

## Morning Market Outlook

### Previous US Session

The S&P 500 ended essentially unchanged at 7,764.64. The Nasdaq gained 0.45% to a record 27,244.28, while the Dow fell 0.36% to 51,863.69. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.38% to 12,604.20 after trading between 12,338.52 and 12,604.26, closing near its session high. The VIX declined to 14.21. **Stable headline indexes concealed continued strength in technology and semiconductors alongside pressure on the Dow and banks, showing that capital remained concentrated in growth and artificial-intelligence exposure rather than broad risk expansion.**

Technically, the Nasdaq and semiconductor index retained rising trends, and the semiconductor index's close near the daily high gave it stronger short-term momentum than the broad market. The S&P 500 consolidated after the prior session's rally and remained roughly 0.4% below its earlier record close. Breadth did not match technology's strength, leaving a clear relative-strength signal but incomplete evidence of a broad breakout. Reliable aggregate volume comparisons were not officially confirmed, so price gains were not treated as a price-and-volume confirmation.

The US 10-year Treasury yield stayed near 4.95%, below the recent 5% valuation-pressure threshold. Brent briefly fell below USD 98 per barrel before recovering toward USD 100, indicating that the energy risk premium had eased without disappearing. Semiconductor valuations retained support as long as long yields and oil did not rise together. A renewed advance in both would test the technology trade first because of its concentration.

### Previous Taiwan Session

The Taiex reached an intraday high of 48,601.53 and closed at 47,800.17, up 81.33 points or 0.17%, on turnover of about TWD 1.02 trillion. The close near the session low exposed substantial profit-taking. The OTC index gained 0.63%, but only 330 main-board stocks advanced while 636 declined. TSMC fell 0.81%, while MediaTek, panel makers and substrate companies outperformed. **A record index close accompanied by negative breadth, a weaker TSMC and rotation into smaller electronics represented high-level turnover rather than an indiscriminate bull market.**

The three major institutional groups bought about TWD 60.81 billion. Foreign investors bought TWD 45.32 billion, investment trusts sold about TWD 0.37 billion, and dealers bought about TWD 15.85 billion. Foreign investors held 10,740 long and 86,308 short Taiwan index-futures contracts, a net short of 75,568 contracts that increased by 1,487. The option volume put/call ratio was 101.40%, and the open-interest ratio was 87.33%. Foreign investors added cash exposure while increasing futures hedges, so positioning did not turn uniformly bullish.

Financed shares declined by about 85,100 lots to 9.2454 million lots, while margin-financing value rose by about TWD 1.82 billion to TWD 604.86 billion. Securities-lending short balances declined by roughly 72 million shares. This combination suggested that lower-priced financed positions fell while capital moved into more expensive shares, without a matching expansion in securities-borrowing shorts. Fundamentals still received support from August export orders, which rose 71.4% from a year earlier, but strong demand did not remove the near-term volatility created by concentrated positioning.

### Taiwan Session Outlook

The base case was a constructive but volatile session around 47,800. Semiconductors and the artificial-intelligence supply chain had support from another gain in the US semiconductor index, but the durability of any firm opening depended on TSMC, the OTC market and advancing issues moving together. If the index advanced, OTC relative strength held and foreign futures shorts stopped increasing, the previous session would qualify as healthy turnover. A renewed test of 48,600 with persistently negative breadth would make chasing the breakout riskier than the headline suggested.

### US Pre-Market Outlook

The base case for the next US session was continued technology relative strength and broad-index consolidation near the high. Morning futures data were not yet sufficient to confirm direction, so small Asian-hours moves were not treated as a trend. Confirmation required the semiconductor index to extend, the S&P 500 to clear its prior high, and financials, small caps and consumer discretionary stocks to participate. Without those improvements, the Nasdaq record remained a concentrated-market signal.

### Invalidation Conditions

The constructive Taiwan view would fail if the Taiex broke below 47,800, the OTC market weakened, declining issues continued to expand and foreign futures net shorts exceeded the previous session's 75,568 contracts. The US technology-relative-strength view would fail if the 10-year yield moved back above 5%, Brent rose decisively above USD 100 and the semiconductor index broke below the previous session's 12,338.52 low.

### Taiwan Market

The Taiex gained 357.12 points, or 0.75%, to a record 48,157.29 after trading between 47,894.77 and 48,341.56. Turnover was about TWD 855.95 billion, down from roughly TWD 1.068 trillion in the previous session. Financials fell 0.78%, while artificial-intelligence and large electronic stocks provided the main support. **A close above 47,800 in the upper half of the daily range confirmed that the uptrend remained intact, but lower turnover showed that this was still a concentrated advance rather than broad chasing.**

The intraday low remained above the previous close, but the index gave back about 184 points from the high, showing that overhead supply had not been fully absorbed. This path did not imply an immediate reversal. It made the first hour around 48,000, electronic turnover and stabilization in financials the next tests. If only the largest stocks advanced while the rest of electronics remained divided, the record would offer less tradable strength than its headline gain implied.

Foreign investors bought TWD 37.31 billion, dealers bought TWD 5.83 billion and investment trusts sold TWD 4.22 billion. The three groups together bought TWD 38.92 billion. Continued foreign cash buying aligned with the record index, but investment-trust selling showed that domestic and offshore institutions were not uniformly positioned. The option volume put/call ratio fell to 95.84% and the open-interest ratio fell to 79.83%, indicating less demand for downside protection than one day earlier without proving that risk had disappeared.

The latest official foreign Taiwan-index-futures position, margin balances and securities-lending balances could not be confirmed reliably, so prior-day values were not substituted. The confirmed positioning picture was bullish foreign cash flow, investment-trust selling and lower option protection; whether futures and local leverage moved in the same direction remained unresolved. Initial support was 47,895 to 47,800, with 48,342 as nearby resistance. Taiwan futures markets will be closed Friday and the following Monday, which may continue to restrain pre-holiday turnover.

### Malaysia Market

The FBM KLCI fell 7.07 points, or 0.42%, to 1,676.43 after trading between 1,675.15 and 1,685.37, giving back part of the previous day's advance. Volume declined to 3.20 billion shares from 3.57 billion, while value fell to MYR 2.99 billion from MYR 3.15 billion. Profit-taking in large stocks and lower oil prices weighed on petrochemical and energy names, while the FBM 70 and FBM Emas also retreated. **The index still held near 1,675, but simultaneous weakness in price, turnover and broader indexes showed that the previous rebound had not become a continuous breakout.**

The ringgit ended at 4.0780/4.0825 per US dollar, slightly weaker than 4.0730/4.0775 previously. Bank Negara Malaysia's reference rate was about 4.0692. The currency remained within its recent range, but expectations of further US tightening supported the dollar and prevented the ringgit from benefiting from lower oil. No major domestic release changed the earnings outlook; trading mainly reflected oil, the dollar and position adjustment after the previous session's rebound.

This was not a decline caused by one large constituent: large caps, the FBM 70 and the FBM Emas weakened together as transaction value fell. For foreign investors, a softer ringgit reduced US-dollar returns while energy shares lost oil support. Rotation toward banks, utilities and telecommunications therefore became the key to stabilization near 1,675. A hold above 1,675, a ringgit no weaker than 4.08 and recovering turnover would preserve support from banks, utilities and electronics manufacturing. A break below 1,675 alongside currency weakness would make the week's rebound look more like short covering. Lower oil was negative for energy and petrochemicals but positive for transportation, electricity use and import costs, so sector effects could not be reduced to the index move.

### US Pre-Market and Tonight's Outlook

US equity futures were almost flat: S&P 500 futures rose about 0.06%, Nasdaq 100 futures slipped about 0.01% and Dow futures gained about 0.01%. The US 10-year yield was about 4.957%, the dollar index rose 0.24% to roughly 100.85, WTI fell 0.86% to about USD 89.74 and Brent was nearly unchanged around USD 99.19. The VIX was about 14.17. **A 10-year yield below 5% and oil below USD 100 kept valuation pressure from worsening, while a stronger dollar and directionless futures limited a broader expansion in risk appetite.**

The base case was consolidation near the highs after the open, with technology and semiconductors retaining relative strength but no assumption that the Nasdaq would again lead alone. September purchasing-managers' indexes were the main test. If activity stayed expansionary and the 10-year yield remained below 5%, financials, small caps and consumer discretionary stocks could participate. If the data lifted yields without improving the earnings outlook, high-valuation technology was more exposed to profit-taking.

A flat index and improving risk appetite were different outcomes. The former only required yields and oil to remain below their thresholds; the latter also required advancing issues, equal-weight indexes and cyclical sectors to strengthen. A higher Nasdaq alongside continued Dow and small-cap underperformance after the PMI would extend the concentrated advance. Stable yields with participation from financials and consumer discretionary would provide the breadth confirmation sought in the morning outlook.

### Invalidation Conditions

The constructive Taiwan view would fail if the index moved back below 47,800, foreign investors turned into cash sellers and the OTC market weakened. Malaysia's short-term rebound view would fail if the KLCI broke 1,675, the ringgit weakened beyond 4.08 and turnover expanded. The US base case of technology relative strength and high-level consolidation would fail if the 10-year yield moved above 5%, Brent returned above USD 100 and semiconductor performance weakened together with market breadth.
