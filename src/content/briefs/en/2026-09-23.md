**Primary thesis:** In the morning, US semiconductor strength and improving Malaysian risk appetite supported Taiwan electronics, but narrow US breadth and Taiwan index-futures hedging limited the case for chasing prices. In the evening, Taiwan made another high while Malaysia surrendered part of its rebound; with US yields and oil still below key thresholds, the combined signal favored US consolidation rather than broad risk expansion.

**Secondary signal 1:** Foreign investors were net buyers of Taiwan cash equities while maintaining substantial futures hedges. Option protection eased, but incomplete local leverage data meant stronger prices did not equal uniformly bullish positioning.

**Secondary signal 2:** Malaysia first delivered better price, volume and breadth, then retraced as oil, the dollar and large-cap profit-taking weighed. Regional risk appetite could support electronics manufacturing, but it was not sufficient to confirm a broad rally.

## Morning Market Outlook

### Transmission Thesis

Taiwan entered the session with supportive technology momentum but incomplete broad-market risk appetite. US semiconductor highs directly supported Taiwan's large electronics valuations, while stronger Malaysian equities, currency and turnover added evidence of improving Asian manufacturing sentiment. Taiwan still needed its own price, volume and positioning to confirm the transmission.

### Previous US Signals

The S&P 500 closed at 7,764.64 and was nearly unchanged. The Nasdaq gained 0.45% to 27,244.28, while the Dow fell 0.36% to 51,863.69. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.38% to 12,604.20 and closed near its high; the VIX fell to 14.21. The US 10-year Treasury yield was about 4.95%, and Brent returned toward USD 100 per barrel. **The clearest positive signal for Taiwan was semiconductor relative strength, not a broad US breakout. Weakness in the Dow and banks meant any firm Taiwan opening still required confirmation from local breadth.**

### Previous Malaysia Signals

The FBM KLCI gained 16.56 points, or 0.99%, to 1,683.50 as advancing issues, volume and value improved together. The ringgit strengthened slightly to 4.0730/4.0775 per US dollar. Banks and health care led, while lower oil pressured energy; index futures remained below cash and open interest declined. The session showed that Asian risk recovery was spreading and was constructive for Taiwan electronics manufacturing and regional fund flows. The absence of rebuilt leveraged longs meant Malaysia's rebound could not by itself justify broad Taiwan chasing.

### Taiwan Fundamentals and Technicals

The Taiex reached 48,601.53 intraday and closed at 47,800.17, up 81.33 points or 0.17%, on turnover of about TWD 1.02 trillion. The OTC index gained 0.63%, but only 330 main-board stocks advanced while 636 declined. TSMC fell 0.81%, while MediaTek, panel makers and substrate companies outperformed. **US semiconductor strength and Malaysia's risk recovery supported the opening environment, but Taiwan's close near the low and negative breadth showed that the external impulse was reaching selected electronics rather than the whole market.**

Fundamentals retained support from August export orders, which rose 71.4% from a year earlier, so artificial-intelligence and electronics demand had not reversed. The short-term issue was that much of the good news was already concentrated in expensive leaders. Technically, 47,800 was the first dividing line, while the area around 48,600 represented the previous session's supply. The external technology signal would be confirmed only if TSMC, the OTC market and advancing issues strengthened together. An index-only move would represent high-level rotation rather than a new broad advance.

### Taiwan Positioning

The three major institutional groups bought a combined TWD 60.81 billion: foreign investors bought TWD 45.32 billion, investment trusts sold about TWD 0.37 billion, and dealers bought about TWD 15.85 billion. Foreign investors held 10,740 long and 86,308 short Taiwan index-futures contracts, leaving a net short of 75,568 contracts, 1,487 more than the previous day. The options volume put/call ratio was 101.40%, and the open-interest ratio was 87.33%. **Foreign cash buying absorbed the US technology impulse, but the larger Taiwan index-futures hedge showed that upside exposure still carried tail-risk protection.**

Margin-financed shares fell by about 85,100 lots to 9.2454 million, while margin-financing value rose about TWD 1.82 billion to TWD 604.86 billion, indicating that financed capital was concentrating in higher-priced names. Securities-borrowing short balances declined by roughly 72 million shares, so short exposure did not expand in parallel. Investment trusts trimmed, dealers remained positive and options protection stayed elevated. The key confirmation was therefore not aggregate institutional buying, but whether foreign futures net shorts stopped rising, margin-financing value remained controlled and cash-market breadth improved.

### Taiwan Session Outlook

The base case was a constructive but volatile session above 47,800. Semiconductor and artificial-intelligence suppliers had support from US semiconductor strength, while Malaysia's prior improvement in price and turnover reduced the probability of an abrupt regional risk-off move. If TSMC stabilized, the OTC market held firm, advancing issues expanded and foreign futures net shorts stopped increasing, the previous day's action would qualify as healthy turnover.

If the index tested 48,600 while decliners still dominated, or if margin-financing value and futures net shorts rose together, the external tailwind would only intensify concentration in large caps. In that case, breakout quality would be weaker than the headline index. Taiwan positioning and breadth should take precedence over a single strong US or Malaysian session.

### Invalidation Conditions

The constructive Taiwan view would fail if the Taiex broke below 47,800, the OTC market weakened, declining issues expanded and foreign Taiwan index-futures net shorts exceeded 75,568 contracts. External technology support would also fail if the US 10-year yield moved above 5%, Brent rose decisively above USD 100 and the semiconductor index broke below 12,338.52.

## Evening Market Outlook

### Pre-Market Scorecard

| Morning call | Result | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taiwan constructive above 47,800 | Closed at 48,157.29 | Confirmed |
| Electronics needed volume and breadth confirmation | Index made a high as turnover fell | Partly confirmed |
| Foreign futures hedging needed to stop increasing | Net short increased by 516 to 76,084 | Failed |

Taiwan followed the morning directional base case, but its new high did not come with higher turnover and foreign futures hedging continued to increase. The US technology impulse reached large-cap prices without fully reaching broad risk appetite. The evening question was therefore whether Taiwan's technology resilience and Malaysia's retracement represented demand confirmation for the US, a regional warning, or only enough support for consolidation near the highs.

### Taiwan Closing Signal

The Taiex gained 357.12 points, or 0.75%, to 48,157.29 after trading between 47,894.77 and 48,341.56. Turnover was about TWD 855.95 billion, below the previous day's roughly TWD 1.068 trillion, and financials fell 0.78%. Strength in Taiwan artificial-intelligence and large electronics told the US market that supply-chain demand and valuations had not broken down. Lower turnover and weak financials showed that this was not a broad upgrade to the global growth outlook.

### Taiwan Positioning Confirmation

Foreign investors bought TWD 37.31 billion, dealers bought TWD 5.83 billion and investment trusts sold TWD 4.22 billion, leaving combined buying of TWD 38.92 billion. Foreign investors held 10,855 long and 86,939 short Taiwan index-futures contracts, a net short of 76,084 contracts that increased by 516. The options volume put/call ratio fell to 95.84%, and the open-interest ratio fell to 79.83%. **Cash buying and lower option protection came with a larger futures hedge, so positioning remained divided.**

Margin-financed positions increased by 36,897 lots to 9.2823 million lots, while value rose about TWD 1.51 billion to TWD 606.37 billion. Margin shorts fell by 5,780 lots to 213,059 lots, and the securities-borrowing short-sale balance declined by about 11.61 million shares to 32.34 billion shares. Cash buying and lower short balances were constructive, but higher margin financing and foreign futures net shorts showed that leverage had not turned uniformly bullish.

### Malaysia Closing Signal

The FBM KLCI fell 7.07 points, or 0.42%, to 1,676.43 as volume and value declined. The FBM 70 and FBM Emas also fell, while the ringgit softened to 4.0780/4.0825 per US dollar. Profit-taking in large caps and lower oil weighed on energy and petrochemicals. For the US, this was not a demand collapse; it showed that the Asian risk recovery lacked persistence. The pattern favored continued US technology leadership but argued against assuming that energy, materials and broad cyclicals would take over.

### US Pre-Market Conditions

Taiwan positioning confirmed resilience in the artificial-intelligence supply chain, not a broad expansion in risk appetite. S&P 500 futures were up about 0.06%, Nasdaq 100 futures were down about 0.01% and Dow futures were up about 0.01%, leaving no clear pre-market direction. The US 10-year yield was about 4.957%; the dollar index rose 0.24% to roughly 100.85. WTI fell 0.86% to about USD 89.74, Brent was near USD 99.19 and the VIX was about 14.17. **A 10-year yield below 5% and oil below USD 100 preserved the minimum valuation conditions for technology. A firmer dollar and weak Asian breadth limited the scope for risk appetite to spread.**

Taiwan supplied evidence of semiconductor-chain resilience: another index high and continued foreign cash buying showed that Asian capital had not abandoned core artificial-intelligence assets. Malaysia supplied the cyclical warning: equities, turnover and the currency weakened together, indicating that lower oil and a stronger dollar had not produced broad Asian risk taking. Combined, the signals supported US large technology but left energy, broad cyclicals and the ability of financials to participate unresolved.

### US Session Outlook

The base case was consolidation near the highs, with technology and semiconductors relatively strong but no guarantee that the Nasdaq would again lead by itself. Taiwan's supply-chain price action reduced the risk of an immediate reversal in artificial-intelligence demand, while yields and oil remained below valuation thresholds. If September PMI remained expansionary and the 10-year yield stayed under 5%, large technology would provide the first layer of downside support.

Breadth would determine the quality of the move. If financials, small caps and consumer discretionary shares rose after PMI and the equal-weight index improved, Taiwan's technology resilience could be treated as demand confirmation and the US move could broaden. If the Nasdaq advanced while the Dow and small caps lagged, Taiwan's signal would have transmitted only to US technology and could not be extrapolated into broad US economic strength.

The downside scenario came from discount rates rather than Taiwan demand. If PMI or other data pushed the 10-year yield above 5% while the dollar strengthened further, a Taiwan record would not offset a higher discount rate and expensive semiconductors would be the first area exposed to profit-taking. Oil below USD 100 could ease inflation pressure, but Malaysian weakness in energy and petrochemicals showed that lower oil had uneven earnings effects and was not a universal positive for US sectors.

Trading interpretation must distinguish an index that simply avoids falling from a genuine improvement in US risk appetite. The first requires yields, oil and the VIX to stay within thresholds; the second requires advancing issues, the equal-weight index and cyclical sectors to join. The priority checks were whether the 10-year yield held below 5%, semiconductors extended and financials and small caps improved. If only the first condition held, the move would remain a defensive concentration in technology.

### Invalidation Conditions

The US consolidation case would fail if the 10-year yield moved above 5%, Brent rose back above USD 100, and semiconductor performance and market breadth weakened together. Taiwan's cross-market support would fail if the Taiex fell below 47,800 and foreign investors became cash sellers. Asian risk appetite would need another downgrade if the KLCI broke 1,675 while the ringgit weakened beyond 4.08 per US dollar.
