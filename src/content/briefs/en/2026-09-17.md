## United States

### Confirmed Developments

The Federal Reserve raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00% on September 16, 2026, with a unanimous vote. The statement described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace, job gains as broadly aligned with labor-force growth, and unemployment as little changed, while inflation remained elevated. The market is therefore not dealing with a clean recession signal. It is dealing with resilient growth alongside inflation pressure strong enough to keep policy restrictive.

On implementation, the interest rate on reserve balances was raised to 3.90%, the standing overnight repo rate was set at 4.00%, and the overnight reverse repo offering rate was set at 3.75%. That reprices front-end funding costs and matters directly for equity valuation, credit refinancing and long-duration asset discount rates.

### What the Market Is Pricing

Markets had largely priced a 25-basis-point move before the decision. The surprise was not the hike itself, but the absence of a dovish pivot and the Fed's continued emphasis on inflation. The two-year Treasury yield remained above the upper end of the policy range after the decision, signaling that investors still price either additional tightening or a longer stay at restrictive rates.

The transmission chain is straightforward: sticky inflation and oil risk lift the expected policy path; front-end yields rise; the dollar receives support; high-valuation technology faces a higher discount rate; and credit markets reassess refinancing risk.

### US Market Setup Tonight

**Scenario analysis: base case**

The most likely setup is a choppy and divided US session. Lower oil prices can temporarily reduce inflation anxiety, but the Fed's return to tightening limits valuation expansion. Relative strength is more likely in companies with stable cash flow, pricing power, or a direct benefit from wider rate spreads. Relative weakness is more likely in long-duration growth equities, leveraged companies, unprofitable technology and rate-sensitive real assets.

Confidence: medium. The main drivers are the US two-year and ten-year yields, the dollar, whether oil rebounds, and whether large-cap technology can hold pre-market demand.

**Scenario analysis: bull case**

If the ten-year yield stays below 5%, oil continues to retreat and semiconductor and AI infrastructure shares stabilize, investors may treat the hike as a credible anti-inflation step that reduces the long-end inflation-risk premium. The bullish conditions are a contained dollar, VIX near or below 17, and no widening in credit spreads.

**Scenario analysis: bear case**

If the two-year yield continues higher, the ten-year yield moves back above 5%, and oil rebounds, the market could shift from "the hike is priced" to "policy is threatening growth and credit." The bearish confirmation would be wider high-yield spreads, higher technology volatility and energy prices lifting inflation expectations again.

The base case would be invalidated if long-end yields and oil rise together while credit spreads widen. That would mean the market is moving beyond valuation adjustment and beginning to price funding and liquidity stress.

### Market Impact

Fundamental impact: not yet negative. The Fed's own statement described resilient domestic spending and robust capital investment.

Valuation and discount-rate impact: negative and immediate. Higher policy rates and elevated front-end yields compress long-duration equities and rate-sensitive assets such as data centers and real estate investment trusts.

Liquidity and fund-flow impact: negative for non-US markets if dollar strength persists. Asian currencies and foreign-owned technology markets become more vulnerable.

Credit impact: concentrated in lower-quality borrowers. If rates remain high, the next problem is less an immediate interest-expense shock and more a narrower refinancing window.

### Next Confirmation

The next confirmation points are US labor, inflation and retail-sales data, whether the two-year Treasury yield remains above the policy range, and whether high-yield credit spreads begin to widen.

## Taiwan

### Confirmed Developments

The latest confirmed Taiwan close showed the Taiex rising 337.41 points to 45,848.90 on September 16, 2026, with turnover of NT$643.77 billion. Foreign institutional investors still sold a net NT$17.98 billion on the main board. The rally therefore lacked foreign-flow confirmation and looked more technical and sector-specific than a broad recovery in risk appetite.

The AI server supply chain remained central. Hon Hai and Quanta rose, while financials also gained on the view that higher US rates may support overseas investment income. That suggests the current Taiwan pressure is not an immediate collapse in orders. It is primarily a valuation and foreign-flow issue linked to US rates and the dollar.

### What the Market Is Pricing

Taiwan is pricing two forces. First, the Fed's hike and elevated US yields raise the discount-rate pressure on technology valuations. Second, AI server and semiconductor demand has not yet been proven to have reversed, so investors are still willing to select stocks within the supply chain.

The question is not simply whether US equities fall. The key is whether revenues and orders remain firm for TSMC, AI servers, thermal components, power equipment, printed circuit boards and packaging. If fundamentals hold, weakness is mostly valuation compression. If monthly revenue, investor briefings or customer capex guidance turn lower, the story becomes earnings deterioration.

### Market Impact

Fundamental impact: neutral for now. There is no confirmed break in the AI server chain.

Valuation and discount-rate impact: negative. When the US ten-year yield is near 5%, Taiwan's higher-multiple technology shares face greater valuation pressure.

Liquidity and fund-flow impact: negative. Foreign selling shows that the rebound has not yet been confirmed by offshore capital. The dollar and USD/TWD remain important.

Credit impact: limited for large technology hardware leaders, but smaller suppliers financing expansion with leverage will face a higher cost of capital.

### Next Confirmation

The next confirmation points are Taiwan's central-bank decision, whether foreign investors turn from selling to buying, whether TSMC ADR and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index stabilize, and whether AI server monthly revenue keeps growing.

## Malaysia

### Confirmed Developments

Bank Negara Malaysia kept the overnight policy rate at 2.75% on September 3, 2026. The central bank said Malaysia grew 5.7% in the first half of 2026 and is expected to grow around 5% for the full year, supported by exports, domestic demand, electrical and electronics goods, and tourism spending. Headline and core inflation averaged 1.8% and 2.0% respectively in the first seven months of the year.

Financial-market data showed the ten-year Malaysian government securities yield at 4.17% and the USD/MYR reference rate at 4.0795. Malaysia is therefore not at the center of the rate-hike problem. The more important issue is how higher external rates, energy costs and data-center power demand affect capex and fiscal allocation.

### What the Market Is Pricing

Markets are pricing resilient Malaysian growth but higher external risk. Electrical and electronics exports benefit from the AI and technology cycle, banks benefit from stable rates and credit demand, and utilities and power infrastructure benefit from data-center demand. But if US rates stay high for longer, the ringgit, foreign inflows and long-term funding costs will face pressure.

### Market Impact

Fundamental impact: positive, supported by E&E, tourism, domestic demand and infrastructure.

Valuation and discount-rate impact: neutral to negative. Higher global long-end yields compress REIT, utility and data-center valuations.

Liquidity and fund-flow impact: watch the ringgit and foreign flows. A strong dollar can keep foreign investors conservative.

Credit impact: data centers and power infrastructure are the key areas. If investment expands quickly while tenant quality, contract duration, power cost and utilization remain unclear, credit markets may demand a risk premium before equity markets do.

### Next Confirmation

The next confirmation points are Malaysian government bond yields, the ringgit, bank shares, E&E export orders, and the financing terms for data-center power connections.

## Other Key Markets

China, Japan and Europe are being hit by two shared transmission forces: higher US rates and oil prices that have retreated but remain elevated. If Japanese long-end yields continue to rise, they will add to global bond repricing pressure. In Europe, the combination of energy costs and rates could make industrial and consumer demand the second-order risk.

In energy markets, lower oil reduces near-term inflation anxiety, but supply risk has not disappeared. The US Energy Information Administration reported a smaller-than-expected draw in US commercial crude stocks for the week ended September 11, while gasoline and distillate inventories rose. That pressured oil prices, but Middle East supply and shipping risks remain a potential source of renewed inflation pressure.

## Cross-Market Transmission

The main transmission chain is: the Fed hike and sticky inflation lift front-end rate expectations; the dollar receives support; Asian currencies weaken; and foreign investors become more selective toward Taiwan and Malaysia technology and yield assets. If oil rises again, the issue shifts from valuation compression toward cost inflation and credit pressure.

The second-order effect is a higher funding cost for data centers, power, utilities and leveraged infrastructure. The third-order risk is that equity markets still treat AI infrastructure as a growth story while credit markets start demanding clearer evidence on tenant quality, utilization, secured power and refinancing plans.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. The US two-year yield rises further and markets price more tightening.
2. The US ten-year yield moves back above 5%, deepening valuation compression in high-multiple technology.
3. Oil rebounds on Middle East supply or shipping risk, lifting inflation expectations.
4. Foreign investors keep selling Taiwan equities, leaving the technology rebound without flow confirmation.
5. Data-center financing shifts from an equity growth story to a credit story, widening spreads for weaker projects.

## What the Market May Be Missing

Markets may still be treating AI infrastructure mostly as a revenue-growth theme, but the next stage of pricing may shift toward power access, utilization, debt cost and tenant credit. Data-center capacity must be separated into announced, approved, power-secured, financing-secured, under-construction, energized, pre-leased, operational and actually utilized. If announced capacity is treated as utilized capacity, valuation and credit risk are understated.
