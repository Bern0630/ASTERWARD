## Taiwan Pre-Market

### Confirmed Developments

The Federal Reserve voted 12-0 on September 16 to raise the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%. It described economic activity as solid, domestic spending as resilient, productivity growth as strong and capital investment as robust, while inflation remained elevated. The interest rate on reserve balances rose to 3.90%, the standing overnight repo rate to 4.00%, and the overnight reverse repo offering rate to 3.75%. **This is a resilient-growth tightening, not a recessionary rate increase**.

US equities showed substantial internal dispersion. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81, the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 1.21% to 51,461.90, and the Nasdaq Composite was nearly unchanged. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gained about 0.6%, NVIDIA rose 0.82%, and TSMC ADR gained about 1.2%. Investors did not interpret the hike as a reason to sell every risk asset equally; they differentiated among cash-flow quality, capital intensity and valuation duration.

Rates sent the more important signal. During Asian hours, the two-year Treasury yield was near 4.7145%, the ten-year near 4.9917%, and the thirty-year near 5.3328%. The front end rose more than the long end, flattening the curve. The market is giving the Fed some credit for containing inflation, while recognizing that restrictive rates will eventually constrain growth. The Dollar Index reached approximately 100.33, a seven-week high. VIX closed the prior session at 17.71, elevated but not consistent with market panic.

Credit has not yet confirmed systemic stress. The latest available readings put the US investment-grade option-adjusted spread near 80 basis points and high yield near 276 basis points. These series lag by one to two days, but they indicate that the current shock is still led by the risk-free rate rather than an uncontrolled credit repricing.

### Market Pricing

During Asian trading, rate futures implied roughly a 50% probability of another hike in October, with a December move close to fully priced. That is consistent with the Fed's year-end median policy-rate projection near 4.1%. The surprise was not the September hike. It was the shift from a one-off insurance move toward a tightening cycle that could include consecutive meetings or three cumulative increases.

The transmission sequence is: oil and services inflation keep price pressure elevated; the expected policy path rises; two-year yields and the dollar move first; a ten-year yield near 5% restrains equity multiples; and only a subsequent widening in high-yield spreads would carry the shock from valuation into credit and employment.

AI and semiconductors are following a second path. NVIDIA, TSMC ADR and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose on the hike day, so the market has not confirmed a reversal in AI end demand. Higher rates nevertheless force investors to move beyond capex growth and examine cloud utilization, inference demand, power access and investment payback. Revenue growth can continue to support leaders; long-duration concepts without cash flow are more exposed.

### Market Impact

**Fundamental impact:** Neutral for now. The Fed and recent retail and labor signals do not show a confirmed demand break, and there is no verified reversal in AI chip or advanced-node demand.

**Valuation and discount-rate impact:** Clearly negative. A two-year yield near 4.71% and a ten-year near 5% reduce the present value of long-duration equities, real estate, data centers and capital-intensive projects.

**Liquidity and fund-flow impact:** A seven-week dollar high pressures Asian currencies and foreign-owned markets. The rebound in US futures nevertheless indicates that this is not yet broad deleveraging.

**Credit impact:** Localized rather than systemic. Investment-grade and high-yield spreads remain contained, but refinancing costs are higher. Lower-quality borrowers and pre-cash-flow infrastructure projects require the most scrutiny.

### Next Confirmation

The first test is whether resilient labor data and higher Philadelphia Fed price indexes push the two-year yield above its post-decision high. The second is whether the ten-year yield can remain below 5%. The third is whether high-yield spreads widen materially from approximately 276 basis points. A higher front end with high oil and stable spreads would reinforce hike pricing. A synchronized rise in long yields, the dollar and spreads would confirm a deeper risk-asset correction.

## Taiwan Post-Market and US Pre-Market

### Pre-Market Scorecard

**Confirmed: the Fed hike compressed valuation before producing any immediate credit disorder.** The curve flattened and the dollar strengthened, while investment-grade and high-yield spreads remained relatively contained. The market was still repricing the policy path rather than a systemic credit event.

**Confirmed: cash-generating semiconductors and large technology names retained relative strength.** TSMC and major AI-hardware suppliers lifted the Taiex, but the OTC index fell, showing that leadership did not broaden across the market.

**Partly confirmed: cash-equity buying did not remove macro hedges.** Foreign investors turned net buyers of Taiwan stocks while increasing their net-short index-futures position. Price improvement received partial confirmation, but portfolio protection remained in place.

### Confirmed Developments

**US pre-market data**

S&P 500 futures were about 0.5% higher, Nasdaq futures about 0.6% higher, and Dow futures were also positive. Brent traded near $105.05, while WTI had settled the prior session near $102.05. US initial jobless claims fell to 196,000 from 206,000, the lowest level since mid-July. The Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index eased from 47.4 to 37.8, but new orders remained firm at 29.2; prices paid rose to 48.6 and prices received to 31.3.

Housing was softer in aggregate. August housing starts fell to a 1.275 million annual rate, down 2.6% on the month and 1.2% on the year. Building permits fell 2.7% to 1.394 million, though they remained 3.5% above a year earlier. Single-family starts increased 7.6% to 918,000. Resilient labor and manufacturing signals, higher price indexes and weaker aggregate housing did not provide a clean case for a rapid dovish turn.

**Taiwan**

The Taiex gained 439.10 points to close at 46,288.00 on September 17, up 0.96%, with turnover of approximately NT$819.405 billion. It rose as much as 1,025.94 points intraday to 46,874.84 before surrendering more than half the gain. There were 772 advancers and 251 decliners, but the OTC index fell 0.26%. This was a large-cap and supply-chain rebound, not an unconditional return of risk appetite.

TSMC rose 1.89% to NT$2,425, Hon Hai gained to NT$250.5, and UMC rose 3.87%. MediaTek and Delta Electronics fell to NT$4,500 and NT$1,685 respectively. The prior US session's gains in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, NVIDIA and TSMC ADR provided support beyond valuation relief. TSMC's August revenue of NT$514.806 billion, up 53.3% year on year, remains the clearest fundamental anchor.

The index alone does not settle the fund-flow question. USD/TWD traded near 31.88 during the morning, and a firm dollar can still discourage offshore investors from chasing the rally. Post-close official data nevertheless show foreign cash investors reversing the prior day's selling and supporting the large-cap rebound. The negative OTC close and selling in selected smaller suppliers show that the improvement did not spread across the full market.

**Malaysia**

At midday, the FBM KLCI was down 3.30 points at 1,675.91, a decline of about 0.2%. Market breadth was weaker, with 650 decliners against 380 advancers. Petronas Chemicals and Malaysian Pacific Industries both fell, showing why high oil prices should not be treated as uniformly positive for energy and electronics: upstream oil and gas, petrochemical margins and technology demand require separate analysis.

The ringgit opened weaker at 4.0855-4.0910 per US dollar after the Fed decision. The ten-year Malaysian government securities yield was approximately 4.218% in the prior session, around four basis points above September 15. Bank Negara Malaysia held the OPR at 2.75% on September 3. Official data showed second-quarter GDP growth of 6.0% and first-half growth of 5.7%, while headline and core inflation averaged 1.8% and 2.0% respectively in the first seven months.

### Positioning and Flows

TWSE post-close data show foreign investors bought a net NT$12.185 billion. Dealers bought NT$5.894 billion in proprietary books and sold NT$4.316 billion in hedging books, while investment trusts bought NT$7.714 billion. The three institutional groups together bought NT$21.477 billion. Compared with foreign selling of NT$17.980 billion on September 16, **the one-day reversal in foreign cash flow was approximately NT$30.165 billion**. That confirms support for large caps, but does not erase the intraday fade or weaker OTC market.

At the stock level, foreign investors bought 8.880 million Hon Hai shares, 8.826 million Wistron, 4.790 million Quanta, 4.794 million TSMC and 2.463 million ASE Technology. They also bought 0.300 million MediaTek shares. Delta Electronics still saw 0.663 million shares of foreign selling. Wiwynn received only 0.065 million shares of foreign buying while investment trusts sold 0.581 million, and Unimicron received 0.807 million of foreign buying while investment trusts sold 1.519 million. Foreign investors rebuilt exposure to large hardware and foundry leaders, while domestic institutions remained more selective in high-priced servers and PCBs.

In index futures, foreign investors held 8,161 long and 86,835 short open contracts, for a net short position of 78,674 contracts, 2,323 contracts more net short than on September 16. Cash buying alongside greater futures hedging means equity exposure was rebuilt without removing macro protection. The Taiwan index-options volume put/call ratio was 107.24%, while the open-interest ratio was 76.50%. Short-term trading favored protection, but the stock of options positions was less bearish than the prior day.

Credit and leverage data show that Listed-market margin financing rose from NT$586.169 billion to NT$589.397 billion, an increase of NT$3.229 billion. Financed share units increased from 9.249 million lots to 9.262 million lots, a gain of 13,145 lots. Margin short-selling balances rose from 197,348 to 216,288 lots, an increase of 18,940 lots. Long and short leverage expanded together: the rebound attracted financed buying while also increasing hedging and short demand.

TWSE's aggregate securities-lending overview shows total borrowed balances declining from 25.877 billion shares on September 16 to 25.781 billion on September 17, a decrease of approximately 95.746 million shares. Balances in the TWSE lending system fell, while balances held through securities firms and finance companies rose modestly. Aggregate borrowing did not increase with foreign futures net shorts, suggesting that the day's defensive positioning was expressed mainly through index futures and options rather than a broad increase in single-stock borrowing.

### Market Pricing

**Taiwan**

The market is separating earnings deterioration from multiple compression. TSMC revenue, advanced-node demand and AI server orders still support the medium-term fundamentals of TSMC, Hon Hai, Quanta, Wistron, Wiwynn, ASE, MediaTek and Delta, as well as PCB, thermal and power-equipment suppliers. A US ten-year yield near 5% and a firm dollar nevertheless reduce acceptable valuation multiples.

The fade from the intraday high and the negative OTC close show that investors are not buying the entire market. If TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and NVIDIA remain firm while smaller Taiwan AI suppliers lag, the adjustment is primarily valuation and positioning. If monthly revenue, orders or hyperscaler capex guidance weaken, the issue becomes fundamental.

Taiwan's central bank kept the discount rate at 2%, the rate on accommodations with collateral at 2.375%, and the rate on accommodations without collateral at 4.25%. It raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 11.48% and projected full-year CPI inflation of 2.03%. The decision did not mechanically follow the Fed; it balanced export and AI-investment strength against inflation still slightly above 2%.

Selective credit controls were eased at the margin. The bank raised the second-home mortgage LTV cap from 60% to 70%, effective September 18, and removed the written-undertaking requirement that land-loan borrowers begin construction within a prescribed period. This was not broad easing. It combined unchanged policy rates with targeted relief for housing credit, directly affecting how banks, developers and asset-backed equities are priced.

**Malaysia**

Malaysia's central tension is not an imminent domestic rate increase. It is the coexistence of higher US rates, a firmer dollar and elevated energy prices. Banks benefit from a stable OPR and credit growth, but rising government-bond yields create securities-valuation and funding pressure. Upstream oil and gas and palm oil receive commodity-price support, while downstream petrochemicals, airlines, transport and consumers face higher costs.

E&E exports remain supported by global technology spending, but weakness in Malaysian Pacific Industries is a reminder that AI growth will not distribute evenly across electronics companies. Construction, utilities, power equipment, data centers and industrial property have order visibility. REITs and leveraged developers are more sensitive to the ten-year government yield and refinancing cost.

Weak Chinese and Hong Kong equities today show that China demand is not yet providing a clear tailwind. Malaysia will release August consumer prices and trade data tomorrow. Those reports will test whether high oil remains primarily an export and fiscal support, or is becoming a broader domestic cost shock.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Scenario analysis: base case**

The expected direction is an early gain followed by a choppy session, with medium confidence. Positive futures, a ten-year yield temporarily below 5%, and oil retreating from its peak support large-cap technology and semiconductors. The two-year yield, the dollar and the repricing of the next Fed move still cap index-level upside. Relative strength is most likely in semiconductors, optical and network interconnect, cash-generating AI infrastructure, and selected large banks. Relative weakness is more likely in leveraged small caps, unprofitable software, REITs and rate-sensitive housing exposures.

The key levels are 5% on the ten-year yield, 100.3 on the Dollar Index, 18 on VIX, and $105-$107 on Brent. After the premarket releases, if credit spreads remain contained, part of the premarket gain can hold, but broad multiple expansion would still be difficult to justify.

**Scenario analysis: bull case**

If markets treat softer aggregate housing as evidence that policy is beginning to cool activity, the ten-year yield falls below 4.95%, oil extends its retreat, and VIX stays below 17.5, the market may treat the hike as a restoration of policy credibility rather than a growth shock. Semiconductors, cloud equipment, communications and high-quality growth would lead. Banks would depend on the curve avoiding further flattening.

**Scenario analysis: bear case**

If low jobless claims and firmer survey price indexes raise hike pricing, the two-year yield breaks above its post-decision high, the ten-year moves above 5.05%, or Brent returns above $107, investors will raise the odds of an October hike. If VIX simultaneously moves above 19 and high-yield spreads widen, the selloff would expand from valuation compression to credit risk. Small caps, real estate, leveraged consumer exposures and unprofitable technology would be weakest.

The base case is invalidated if long-end yields, the dollar, oil and credit spreads rise together. A higher two-year yield with stable long yields and spreads is still a policy-path adjustment. A simultaneous rise in all four would indicate that liquidity and credit stress are reinforcing each other.

### Market Impact

**Taiwan**

**Fundamental impact:** Neutral to positive. TSMC's August revenue growth and relative resilience in US semiconductors do not support a conclusion that AI orders have broken.

**Valuation and discount-rate impact:** Negative. A US long yield near 5% weighs most heavily on high-multiple suppliers whose earnings are further in the future.

**Liquidity and fund-flow impact:** Neutral to positive but divided. Foreign cash investors bought NT$12.185 billion and the three institutions bought NT$21.477 billion, supporting large caps. Foreign futures net shorts nevertheless rose to 78,674 contracts, indicating that cash rebuilding still carried macro protection.

**Credit impact:** Limited for large technology leaders. Smaller thermal, power-equipment, PCB and facility suppliers financing rapid expansion with short-dated or floating-rate debt should be judged by interest coverage, not order value alone.

**Malaysia**

**Fundamental impact:** Still positive overall, supported by second-quarter GDP, domestic demand and E&E exports. Airlines, downstream petrochemicals and household purchasing power face energy-cost pressure.

**Valuation and discount-rate impact:** Neutral to negative. A ten-year government yield near 4.22% raises required cap rates for REITs, utilities, data centers and construction assets.

**Liquidity and fund-flow impact:** Negative. The ringgit weakened after the Fed decision, reducing currency-hedged returns for foreign investors. Midday market breadth also showed a defensive tone.

**Credit impact:** Concentrated in leveraged, long-payback projects. Data-center and grid investments with long leases, strong tenants, secured power and fixed-rate funding remain manageable. Projects with announced capacity but no energized or pre-leased capacity are most exposed.

Watch how markets price unchanged rates and the higher second-home LTV cap, especially the relative performance of banks, developers and asset-backed equities. Also test whether TSMC ADR and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index hold for a second day, whether USD/TWD breaks 32, and whether foreign cash buying persists while futures net shorts decline. **Malaysia**

Tomorrow's August CPI and trade releases are the first tests. The next are whether USD/MYR stays below 4.10, whether the ten-year government yield continues higher, and whether clearer relative strength emerges among banks, E&E, utilities, data centers, REITs and upstream oil and gas.

## Other Key Markets

During Asian trading, Japanese equities were approximately 0.5% higher, while markets almost fully expected the Bank of Japan to raise rates on September 18. If Japanese long yields continue higher, repatriation into yen assets could accelerate. Chinese blue chips fell about 0.4% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost about 0.9%, showing that Asian risk appetite did not improve uniformly.

The Bank of England kept Bank Rate at 3.75% by a 6-3 vote, with three members preferring 4.00%. UK CPI inflation was 3.1%, leaving policy risks tilted further to the upside. The Bank also unanimously adopted a multi-year plan to reduce its government-bond stock to zero, combining £20 billion of annual active sales with maturities for an average unwind of about £46 billion a year through 2034. European assets therefore face not only policy rates but the combination of high energy costs, inflation and quantitative tightening. Brent's retreat to approximately $105.05 reduced immediate supply anxiety without removing it.

## Cross-Market Transmission

The first chain is: Fed tightening lifts the two-year yield, flattens the curve and strengthens the dollar, which pressures Asian currencies and foreign-investor returns. Taiwan absorbs it through high-multiple technology and offshore positioning. Malaysia absorbs it through the ringgit, government-bond yields and long-duration infrastructure valuations.

The second chain is: oil above $100 supports energy exporters' fiscal positions and upstream producers, but raises imported inflation, transport costs and the risk of further central-bank tightening. Taiwan bears cost and currency pressure. Malaysia has an energy-export buffer, but subsidies, generation fuel and end-user pricing still create a fiscal allocation problem.

The third chain is: AI capex continues to support semiconductor and server orders, but higher discount rates shift attention to cash flow and credit. Equity investors may reward revenue growth first. Credit investors demand pre-leasing, secured power, parent guarantees, debt-service coverage and refinancing plans. Divergence between the two markets is itself an early risk signal.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. The US ten-year yield moves back above 5.05% while the dollar and oil rise with it.
2. The probability of an October hike rises sharply from approximately 50%, invalidating the US futures rebound.

4. Taiwan's index rebound fails to receive confirmation from foreign flows and smaller companies, causing the large-cap rally to fade again.
5. Malaysian data-center and power investment treats announced capacity as energized, pre-leased or actually utilized capacity, creating a capex and credit mismatch.

## What the Market May Be Missing

The first underappreciated signal is the combination of a flatter yield curve and still-contained credit spreads. That configuration hurts valuation before it necessarily hurts earnings. Indices can therefore remain volatile rather than decline in a straight line, while cash-generating semiconductor and infrastructure suppliers retain relative strength. The true regime shift is not the ten-year yield crossing 5% by itself. It is a simultaneous rise in long-end yields, the dollar, oil and credit spreads.

The second blind spot is the definition of data-center capacity. Malaysia's signed maximum electricity demand, planned or under-construction capacity, energized capacity, pre-leased capacity and actual load are not interchangeable. Tracking announced megawatts alone overstates near-term cash flow and understates grid investment and refinancing needs.
