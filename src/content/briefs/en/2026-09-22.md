## Taiwan Pre-Market

### Confirmed Developments

US equities rallied across the major indexes on Monday. The S&P 500 gained 1.49% to 7,764.70, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.71% to 52,048.83, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 2.26% to 27,122.09 and the Russell 2000 added 0.52% to 2,875.36. Large technology companies drove the move, but participation was not limited to one stock: eight of the S&P 500's 11 sectors advanced and gainers outnumbered decliners by about 1.4 to one.

Semiconductors were the strongest theme. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 4.3%, NVIDIA gained 2.3% and TSMC ADRs increased 2.41% to USD 445.14. AMD jumped 9.9%, while Intel and Arm rose even more. **This price set provides clear opening support for Taiwan's large semiconductor companies, but the Nasdaq still recorded more new lows than new highs, so a chip rally is not yet evidence that risk has cleared across all growth equities.**

Cross-asset pricing also supported the valuation rebound. Two-, 10- and 30-year Treasury yields closed at 4.76%, 4.96% and 5.29%, respectively, with the 10-year easing from about 5.01% in the prior session. Brent crude fell 3.4% to USD 100.34 a barrel, while the Dollar Index was near 99.98. Lower oil and long yields reduced inflation and discount-rate pressure, but a 10-year yield close to 5% still represents tight financial conditions rather than easing.

Risk appetite carried into early Asian trading. The MSCI Asia Pacific Index gained about 0.7%, S&P 500 futures rose roughly 0.4% and Nasdaq 100 futures added about 0.6%, while mainland Chinese equities advanced. Japan is in a three-day holiday period, leaving cash rates and equity liquidity thin as the yen trades near 156.85 per dollar. The United States and China agreed to establish an artificial-intelligence dialogue, but the duration of any trade truce, technology restrictions and purchase commitments still require bilateral documentation.

### Positioning and Flows

Taiwan's Taiex gained 538.09 points, or 1.14%, in the prior session to close at 47,718.84, near its intraday high of 47,750.98, on TWD 820.22 billion of turnover. TSMC rose 0.81% to TWD 2,480, MediaTek advanced 6.37% to TWD 5,010 and Delta Electronics gained 8.07%, while the Taipei Exchange slipped 0.03% to 412.57. **A main-board close near the high without a matching OTC gain shows that price confirmation remained concentrated in large technology, display and financial shares rather than broadening across the market.**

The three institutional groups bought a net TWD 47.04 billion on the main board, including TWD 20.38 billion from foreign investors, TWD 3.93 billion from investment trusts and TWD 22.73 billion from dealers. Foreign investors held 10,318 long and 84,399 short Taiex futures contracts, leaving a net short of 74,081 contracts, 2,029 fewer than one session earlier. The options put/call volume ratio was 125.10%, while the open-interest ratio was 96.20%. Cash buying and futures covering were positive, but option turnover retained meaningful downside protection.

Domestic leverage accelerated. Main-board margin financing rose about TWD 9.00 billion from TWD 594.04 billion to TWD 603.04 billion, while financed shares increased by 53,272 lots to 9.33 million. Short-financed shares rose by 8,619 lots to 231,582. The securities-borrowing short-sale balance declined from about 32.443 billion shares to 32.427 billion, with roughly 379 million shares sold and 394 million returned during the session. **Institutional cash buying and fewer futures shorts support the advance, but margin financing grew faster than the evidence of broad turnover, increasing today's burden of confirmation.**

### Market Pricing

The base case is a higher open led by large semiconductors, followed by a test of market breadth. Gains in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, NVIDIA and TSMC ADRs directly support TSMC, advanced process and packaging, ASIC, server-power and high-speed transmission suppliers. MediaTek and Delta already rose sharply on Monday, however, so imbalanced volume could turn positive news into an opportunity to reduce positions rather than chase prices.

Relative strength matters more than the index's absolute move. The Taiex gained 1.14% while the Taipei Exchange was nearly flat, showing that investors were willing to pay a premium for large, liquid companies with demonstrable cash flow without extending the same valuation expansion to smaller electronics shares. **The genuinely bullish signal today would not be another index high; it would be simultaneous improvement in the OTC market, advancing-share breadth and turnover participation.**

Malaysia offers a second test of Asian risk appetite. The FBM KLCI rose only 0.08% to 1,666.94 on Monday as trading value fell to MYR 2.8 billion and the ringgit strengthened modestly. Financials, refining and selected renewable-energy shares offset weakness in technology, construction and industrial large caps. Lower oil helps airlines, consumers and imported costs but removes part of the premium from upstream energy. Electrical and electronics companies and data centers still need volume and foreign-flow evidence that demand can become shareholder returns.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** The 10-year Treasury yield stays below 5% and Brent remains in a USD 100 to USD 102 range. Technology and semiconductors retain relative leadership, but the indexes consolidate after Monday's surge. Breadth improves modestly and the Russell 2000 does not materially underperform.

**Bull case:** US-China officials provide executable details on a trade-truce extension or AI dialogue, oil falls below USD 100 and the 10-year yield moves toward 4.90%. Industrials, financials, smaller companies and consumer discretionary stocks join semiconductors, confirming broader risk appetite.

**Bear case:** Oil rebounds, the 10-year yield returns above 5%, or pre-summit language raises the risk of tighter technology controls. If the semiconductor index gives back an early gain while Nasdaq new lows continue to exceed new highs, Monday's move would look more like a valuation repair in crowded positions than a new market-wide advance.

### Market Impact

**Taiwan:** External price confirmation is strongest for large semiconductors. Yet a roughly TWD 9.0 billion rise in margin financing and a weaker Taipei Exchange concentrate chasing risk in volatile smaller companies. Continued foreign cash buying, fewer futures shorts and a stronger OTC market must appear together to upgrade the move from concentrated leadership to broad participation.

**Malaysia:** Lower oil reduces costs for airlines, manufacturers and data centers but compresses the premium for upstream energy. Stable global chip shares and the ringgit would support electrical and electronics companies. If turnover stays low and foreign capital does not return, index gains are more likely to remain selective.

**United States:** Lower oil and long yields improve valuations for long-duration technology and high-quality growth, but a 4.76% two-year yield and 4.96% 10-year yield remain restrictive. Investors are again assigning more weight to AI growth, not removing constraints from funding costs, credit and earnings delivery.

### Next Confirmation

After Taiwan opens, first test whether TSMC, MediaTek and Delta can absorb demand without disorderly volume, then whether the Taipei Exchange, advancing shares and smaller electronics companies strengthen together. If the Taiex opens higher while OTC breadth remains weak, the move should be classified as transmission from US chip prices rather than a broad rise in Taiwan risk appetite.

The second test is foreign cash flow, the Taiex futures basis and the Taiwan dollar. Continued foreign buying, a futures net short below 74,081 contracts and a stable currency would indicate that overseas investors are still adding exposure. If cash turns negative, futures shorts rebuild and margin financing continues to rise, institutional trend demand will have given way to local leveraged support.

The third test now has a partial answer. Taiwan's August export orders reached USD 102.96 billion, rising 5.1% month over month and 71.4% year over year, while January-August orders increased 55.0%. The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate edged down from 3.33% to 3.32%. **External demand is still being lifted by AI and high-performance computing orders, but the labor market is stable rather than accelerating at the same rate as exports.**

## Taiwan Market

### Confirmed Developments

The Taiex closed at 47,800.17, up 81.33 points or 0.17%, on approximately TWD 1.079 trillion of turnover. It opened at 47,981.04, reached 48,601.53 and finished at the session low. The Taipei Exchange gained 0.63% to 415.17 on roughly TWD 282.11 billion. **An 801-point retreat from the Taiex high alongside an OTC gain shows simultaneous profit-taking in large caps and rotation into smaller electronics, rather than wholesale risk reduction.**

There were 330 listed gainers, 636 decliners and 113 unchanged shares. The semiconductor sub-index added only 0.08%, while financials fell 0.67%. TSMC declined 0.81% to TWD 2,460, MediaTek rose 3.39% to TWD 5,180 and Delta Electronics gained 1.33% to TWD 1,900. AUO hit its daily limit at TWD 36.65 on more than 1.16 billion shares, while Unimicron and Nanya PCB also closed limit-up. **Leadership shifted from TSMC alone toward MediaTek, displays, substrates and selected testing names.**

August export orders reached USD 102.96 billion, up 5.1% month over month, 5.9% after seasonal adjustment and 71.4% year over year. January-August orders totaled USD 704.99 billion, up 55.0%. These data confirm expanding AI and high-performance computing demand, but orders lead future shipments and cannot be treated as current-quarter revenue or free cash flow.

Labor conditions remained stable. The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate slipped to 3.32% from 3.33%, employment was about 11.648 million and labor-force participation was near 59.59%; the unadjusted rate increased to 3.41% from 3.39%. Rapid orders alongside little change in unemployment show that external strength remains concentrated in capital- and technology-intensive industries.

### Positioning and Flows

The three institutional groups bought a net TWD 60.81 billion on the main board. Foreign investors bought TWD 45.32 billion, dealers bought TWD 15.85 billion and investment trusts sold TWD 0.37 billion. Foreign buying concentrated in AUO, UMC, Innolux and King Yuan Electronics, while Wistron and Nanya Technology were reduced. **The flow had clear industry selection and did not lift every part of the AI supply chain together.**

Foreign investors held 10,740 long and 86,308 short Taiex futures contracts, leaving a net short of 75,568, an increase of 1,487. The options volume put/call ratio was 101.40% and the open-interest ratio was 87.33%. Cash buying with more index shorts is best read as higher stock exposure with retained tail protection, not a one-way bullish position.

Margin-financed shares fell by about 85,100 lots to 9.245 million, but the balance increased by roughly TWD 1.82 billion to TWD 604.86 billion. Short-financed shares declined by about 12,700 lots, while the securities-borrowing short-sale balance fell by roughly 72.4 million shares to 32.355 billion. **Lower share counts but a higher financing value show that leverage is being cleaned up while remaining concentrated in higher-priced leaders.**

### Market Pricing

The market is separating order growth, price leadership and cash-flow delivery rather than rejecting AI demand. Export orders provide fundamental support, but TSMC's reversal and negative listed breadth show that a near-5% Treasury yield still constrains expensive large caps. Strength in MediaTek, substrates and displays shows investors searching for earnings elasticity not fully priced.

The next signal is whether 47,800 holds without another increase in foreign futures shorts. TSMC stabilization, continued OTC relative strength and better listed breadth would define Tuesday as healthy rotation. More futures shorts alongside a higher margin balance would turn 48,601 into a nearer-term supply zone.

### Market Impact

For large semiconductors, export orders and US chip prices confirm medium-term demand, but high yields require companies to convert orders into gross margin and free cash flow. For displays, substrates and testing companies, heavy-volume gains improve trend visibility while raising next-session reversal risk.

For non-electronics, oil below USD 100 helps transport, airlines and manufacturing costs but reduces the energy and materials risk premium. Weaker financials alongside lower Treasury yields show that markets are trading lower funding pressure rather than faster broad growth.

### Next Confirmation

Continued foreign cash buying with net shorts below 74,081 would realign institutional direction. A smaller cash bid with net shorts above 76,000 would define Tuesday's flow as stock selection and covering rather than an index commitment.

Wednesday's industrial-production and retail-sales releases provide the next fundamental test. Orders confirm future demand; production must show actual delivery, while retail sales test whether exports and asset prices are reaching domestic demand.

## Malaysia Market

### Confirmed Developments

The FBM KLCI gained 16.56 points, or 0.99%, to 1,683.50 after trading between 1,665.23 and 1,684.34. Breadth improved to 678 gainers versus 458 decliners and 572 unchanged. Volume rose to 3.57 billion shares from 2.93 billion, while value increased to MYR 3.15 billion from MYR 2.49 billion. **Price, breadth and activity improved together, making this more than a narrow heavyweight rebound.**

Banks and healthcare provided the largest push. CIMB rose MYR 0.25 to MYR 7.90, IHH Healthcare gained MYR 0.38 to MYR 8.00, and Maybank and Tenaga Nasional each added MYR 0.06. Financials jumped while industrial products and plantations also advanced; energy slipped as oil fell. The mix combines valuation repair, resilient domestic demand and lower energy costs.

The ringgit firmed modestly to 4.0730/4.0775 per dollar from 4.0755/4.0800 and also strengthened against the euro and pound. The measured currency response suggests that equities were driven mainly by bargain hunting and better global technology sentiment, rather than a large cross-border capital repricing.

### Positioning and Flows

September KLCI futures rose 13 points to 1,673.0, still 10.5 points below cash. October futures settled at 1,672.0 as turnover fell to 8,146 contracts from 10,117 and open interest declined to 48,706 from 51,310. **Higher futures with a discount and falling open interest look more like short covering and lower risk than fresh leveraged longs.**

Bank Negara Malaysia reopened MYR 5.0 billion of the 3.844% April 2033 government bond. Bids reached MYR 10.842 billion for a 2.17 bid-to-cover ratio. Demand absorbed supply and stabilized medium-term funding, but the previous day's 10-year MGS reference yield near 4.15% still imposes a meaningful hurdle on corporate funding and data-center capital expenditure.

### Market Pricing

Malaysia is rotating from an energy-premium trade toward domestic financials, healthcare and technology valuation repair. Lower oil helps airlines, manufacturing and consumption costs while reducing upstream earnings expectations. Electrical and electronics and data centers still need foreign inflows and company orders rather than relying only on US chip performance.

### Market Impact

A well-covered government auction reduces sovereign-funding concern, but long yields above 4% still increase data-center project costs. Lower technology-control tail risk from US-China talks could support Penang electronics suppliers; political language without purchase or control details would leave Tuesday's move closer to valuation repair.

### Next Confirmation

Watch whether the FBM KLCI holds 1,670-1,680 and whether the futures discount narrows. Friday's July Leading Index must then extend June's 1.3% annual gain. Better breadth without confirmation from the leading index, export electronics and foreign flows would limit durability.

## United States Market

### Confirmed Developments

US futures were modestly positive before the open: Dow and S&P 500 futures gained less than 0.1%, while Nasdaq futures rose about 0.3%. Brent fell roughly USD 2.05 to USD 98.29, West Texas Intermediate declined about USD 2.38 below USD 90, and the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.93% from about 4.95%.

This was oil's fifth consecutive decline as markets priced the possible restoration of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and Red Sea exports. Crude remains almost USD 30 above its year-earlier level and neither Middle East supply nor Strait of Hormuz transit has fully normalized, so the inflation premium has declined rather than disappeared.

### Market Pricing

A 10-year yield below 5% and Brent below USD 100 reduce discount-rate pressure on technology and help smaller companies and consumers. Nasdaq futures were only modestly higher after Monday's 2.26% rally, showing willingness to retain AI exposure while waiting for summit and oil-supply news.

Alibaba rose 3.1% after unveiling new AI-chip technology as investors awaited US-China decisions on trade, tariffs, artificial intelligence and critical minerals. Executable agreements would benefit US platforms, chip designers and Asian suppliers together; a politically focused summit would keep gains concentrated in a few leaders.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** The 10-year yield holds between 4.90% and 5.00%, Brent remains between USD 97 and USD 101, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq consolidate. Semiconductors retain relative leadership, but profit-taking caps gains after Monday's surge.

**Bull case:** Actual shipments confirm Saudi alternative export routes while the United States and China provide executable details on the truce, AI dialogue and critical minerals. Participation from financials, industrials, smaller companies and consumer discretionary would confirm broader risk appetite.

**Bear case:** Supply reports are denied, oil returns above USD 100, or pre-summit friction emerges around technology controls and Taiwan. A 10-year yield back above 5% alongside semiconductor weakness would force Taiwan's intraday high and Malaysia's rebound to reprice overnight.

### Market Impact

The United States remains the common discount-rate source for all three core markets. Lower oil and long yields support Taiwan electronics and Malaysian banks, airlines and manufacturers. A renewed increase in either would hit expensive Asian technology and capital-intensive data centers first.

### Next Confirmation

Test whether semiconductors remain positive after Monday's 4.3% gain, then whether the Russell 2000, financials and consumer discretionary participate. Brent at USD 100 and the 10-year yield at 5% are the two boundaries; sustained moves below both would provide room for broader gains.

## Global Markets and Research

### Middle East Energy and Shipping

**Event and Facts:** Saudi Arabia is preparing to restore its East-West pipeline and exports from the Red Sea port of Yanbu, helping push Brent near USD 98.29 for a fifth consecutive decline. Strait of Hormuz traffic remains below normal, however, and the regional conflict is unresolved.

**Market Pricing:** Oil below USD 100 reduces the inflation premium and Treasury yields, lifts US technology valuations, and improves energy costs for Taiwanese manufacturing and Malaysian airlines, consumers and data centers. Malaysia's weaker energy sub-index captures the opposite effect on upstream earnings.

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** Supply confirmation requires sustained Yanbu loadings, lower insurance costs and better Hormuz traffic, not only a pipeline announcement. Brent below USD 100 and the US 10-year below 5% would extend valuation repair; renewed disruption would restore cost pressure for Asian manufacturing and Malaysian power-intensive investment.

### China-US Technology and Trade Negotiations

**Event and Facts:** US and Chinese officials have begun summit preparation on trade, artificial intelligence and critical minerals before the leaders' expected September 24 meeting. Alibaba's new AI-chip technology and 3.1% gain are being traded alongside the possibility of lower policy tail risk.

**Market Pricing:** Chinese and Hong Kong equities rose modestly, Nasdaq futures outperformed, Taiwan's MediaTek, substrate and testing shares strengthened, and Malaysian technology turnover improved. These prices reflect lower tail risk rather than known broad order upgrades; detail on export controls, purchases and the truce duration remains incomplete.

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** An executable AI-dialogue mechanism, critical-mineral terms and tariff timeline would affect US chip designers and platforms first, then Taiwanese manufacturing and Malaysian electrical and electronics suppliers. Formal documents, corporate purchases and foreign flows are the next tests. Without changes to controls, Asian chip gains still require orders and margins.

## Cross-Market Transmission

The first chain runs from lower oil to a smaller inflation premium, a 4.96% US 10-year yield, higher semiconductor valuations and stronger TSMC ADRs, then into Taiwan's large electronics companies. It requires oil not to return quickly to USD 105 and the Treasury yield not to move back above 5%.

The second chain runs through the US-China AI and trade dialogue. A lower supply-chain tail risk first improves valuations for US platforms and chip companies, then affects Taiwanese manufacturing, Malaysian electrical and electronics exports and Asian data-center investment. Medium-term confirmation still requires specific truce, export-control and purchase provisions rather than constructive rhetoric.

The third chain combines foreign cash buying in Taiwan, fewer futures shorts and a sharp rise in local margin financing. The first two support the index while the third increases drawdown sensitivity. Broader participation would allow leverage to reinforce the trend; continued OTC weakness would instead concentrate risk in less-liquid shares.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. Taiwan opens higher but the Taipei Exchange, advancing shares and smaller electronics companies fail to participate.
2. Margin financing continues to expand rapidly after a roughly TWD 9.0 billion daily increase while foreign cash buying fades or reverses.
3. The US 10-year yield returns above 5% and Brent moves back above USD 105, compressing high-multiple valuations.
4. Pre-summit disagreement over technology controls or the trade truce weakens the yuan, Asian chip stocks and freight prices together.
5. Taiwan's export-order headline remains high while industry and geographic concentration rises, leading markets to overstate demand breadth.

## What the Market May Be Missing

**Taiwan's main short-term variable has shifted from whether foreigners will buy to whether foreign buying can outrun the increase in local leverage.** Foreign investors bought TWD 20.38 billion in cash equities and covered 2,029 futures shorts, but margin financing increased by about TWD 9.00 billion while the Taipei Exchange failed to follow the Taiex. If foreign demand slows, holding costs and liquidity risk in smaller shares will appear before weakness in the headline index.

A second underappreciated distinction is that a sharp US semiconductor rally is not the same as a healthy entire market. S&P 500 breadth improved, but Nasdaq new lows still exceeded new highs. If Taiwan also produces strong semiconductor prices but weak OTC breadth, both markets will be pointing to capital concentration rather than a broad demand upgrade.
