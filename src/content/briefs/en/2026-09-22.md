## Taiwan Pre-Market

### Confirmed Developments

US equities rallied across the major indexes on Monday. The S&P 500 gained 1.49% to 7,764.70, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.71% to 52,048.83, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 2.26% to 27,122.09 and the Russell 2000 added 0.52% to 2,875.36. Large technology companies drove the move, but participation was not limited to one stock: eight of the S&P 500's 11 sectors advanced and gainers outnumbered decliners by about 1.4 to one.

Semiconductors were the strongest theme. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 4.3%, NVIDIA gained 2.3% and TSMC ADRs increased 2.41% to USD 445.14. AMD jumped 9.9%, while Intel and Arm rose even more. **This price set provides clear opening support for Taiwan's large semiconductor companies, but the Nasdaq still recorded more new lows than new highs, so a chip rally is not yet evidence that risk has cleared across all growth equities.**

Cross-asset pricing also supported the valuation rebound. Two-, 10- and 30-year Treasury yields closed at 4.76%, 4.96% and 5.29%, respectively, with the 10-year easing from about 5.01% in the prior session. Brent crude fell 3.4% to USD 100.34 a barrel, while the Dollar Index was near 99.98. Lower oil and long yields reduced inflation and discount-rate pressure, but a 10-year yield close to 5% still represents tight financial conditions rather than easing.

Risk appetite carried into early Asian trading. The MSCI Asia Pacific Index gained about 0.7%, S&P 500 futures rose roughly 0.4% and Nasdaq 100 futures added about 0.6%, while mainland Chinese equities advanced. Japan is in a three-day holiday period, leaving cash rates and equity liquidity thin as the yen trades near 156.85 per dollar. The United States and China agreed to establish an artificial-intelligence dialogue, but the duration of any trade truce, technology restrictions and purchase commitments still require bilateral documentation.

### Positioning and Flows

Taiwan's Taiex gained 538.09 points, or 1.14%, in the prior session to close at 47,718.84, near its intraday high of 47,750.98, on TWD 820.22 billion of turnover. TSMC rose 0.81% to TWD 2,480, MediaTek advanced 6.37% to TWD 5,010 and Delta Electronics gained 8.07%, while the Taipei Exchange slipped 0.03% to 412.57. **A main-board close near the high without a matching OTC gain shows that price confirmation remained concentrated in large technology, display and financial shares rather than broadening across the market.**

The three institutional groups bought a net TWD 47.04 billion on the main board, including TWD 20.38 billion from foreign investors, TWD 3.93 billion from investment trusts and TWD 22.73 billion from dealers. Foreign investors held 10,318 long and 84,399 short Taiex futures contracts, leaving a net short of 74,081 contracts, 2,029 fewer than one session earlier. The options put/call volume ratio was 125.10%, while the open-interest ratio was 96.20%. Cash buying and futures covering were positive, but option turnover retained meaningful downside protection.

Domestic leverage accelerated. Main-board margin financing rose about TWD 9.00 billion from TWD 594.04 billion to TWD 603.04 billion, while financed shares increased by 53,272 lots to 9.33 million. Short-financed shares rose by 8,619 lots to 231,582. The securities-borrowing short-sale balance declined from about 32.443 billion shares to 32.427 billion, with roughly 379 million shares sold and 394 million returned during the session. **Institutional cash buying and fewer futures shorts support the advance, but margin financing grew faster than the evidence of broad turnover, increasing today's burden of confirmation.**

### Market Pricing

The base case is a higher open led by large semiconductors, followed by a test of market breadth. Gains in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, NVIDIA and TSMC ADRs directly support TSMC, advanced process and packaging, ASIC, server-power and high-speed transmission suppliers. MediaTek and Delta already rose sharply on Monday, however, so imbalanced volume could turn positive news into an opportunity to reduce positions rather than chase prices.

Relative strength matters more than the index's absolute move. The Taiex gained 1.14% while the Taipei Exchange was nearly flat, showing that investors were willing to pay a premium for large, liquid companies with demonstrable cash flow without extending the same valuation expansion to smaller electronics shares. **The genuinely bullish signal today would not be another index high; it would be simultaneous improvement in the OTC market, advancing-share breadth and turnover participation.**

Malaysia offers a second test of Asian risk appetite. The FBM KLCI rose only 0.08% to 1,666.94 on Monday as trading value fell to MYR 2.8 billion and the ringgit strengthened modestly. Financials, refining and selected renewable-energy shares offset weakness in technology, construction and industrial large caps. Lower oil helps airlines, consumers and imported costs but removes part of the premium from upstream energy. Electrical and electronics companies and data centers still need volume and foreign-flow evidence that demand can become shareholder returns.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** The 10-year Treasury yield stays below 5% and Brent remains in a USD 100 to USD 102 range. Technology and semiconductors retain relative leadership, but the indexes consolidate after Monday's surge. Breadth improves modestly and the Russell 2000 does not materially underperform.

**Bull case:** US-China officials provide executable details on a trade-truce extension or AI dialogue, oil falls below USD 100 and the 10-year yield moves toward 4.90%. Industrials, financials, smaller companies and consumer discretionary stocks join semiconductors, confirming broader risk appetite.

**Bear case:** Oil rebounds, the 10-year yield returns above 5%, or pre-summit language raises the risk of tighter technology controls. If the semiconductor index gives back an early gain while Nasdaq new lows continue to exceed new highs, Monday's move would look more like a valuation repair in crowded positions than a new market-wide advance.

### Market Impact

**Taiwan:** External price confirmation is strongest for large semiconductors. Yet a roughly TWD 9.0 billion rise in margin financing and a weaker Taipei Exchange concentrate chasing risk in volatile smaller companies. Continued foreign cash buying, fewer futures shorts and a stronger OTC market must appear together to upgrade the move from concentrated leadership to broad participation.

**Malaysia:** Lower oil reduces costs for airlines, manufacturers and data centers but compresses the premium for upstream energy. Stable global chip shares and the ringgit would support electrical and electronics companies. If turnover stays low and foreign capital does not return, index gains are more likely to remain selective.

**United States:** Lower oil and long yields improve valuations for long-duration technology and high-quality growth, but a 4.76% two-year yield and 4.96% 10-year yield remain restrictive. Investors are again assigning more weight to AI growth, not removing constraints from funding costs, credit and earnings delivery.

### Next Confirmation

After Taiwan opens, first test whether TSMC, MediaTek and Delta can absorb demand without disorderly volume, then whether the Taipei Exchange, advancing shares and smaller electronics companies strengthen together. If the Taiex opens higher while OTC breadth remains weak, the move should be classified as transmission from US chip prices rather than a broad rise in Taiwan risk appetite.

The second test is foreign cash flow, the Taiex futures basis and the Taiwan dollar. Continued foreign buying, a futures net short below 74,081 contracts and a stable currency would indicate that overseas investors are still adding exposure. If cash turns negative, futures shorts rebuild and margin financing continues to rise, institutional trend demand will have given way to local leveraged support.

The third test is Taiwan's August export-orders and unemployment releases. Export orders rose 61.9% year over year in July, creating a demanding base and reflecting strong AI orders. The important question is not only the headline amount, but whether information and communications products, electronics and major ordering regions retain breadth. Unemployment will test whether export strength is reaching domestic labor demand.

## Cross-Market Transmission

The first chain runs from lower oil to a smaller inflation premium, a 4.96% US 10-year yield, higher semiconductor valuations and stronger TSMC ADRs, then into Taiwan's large electronics companies. It requires oil not to return quickly to USD 105 and the Treasury yield not to move back above 5%.

The second chain runs through the US-China AI and trade dialogue. A lower supply-chain tail risk first improves valuations for US platforms and chip companies, then affects Taiwanese manufacturing, Malaysian electrical and electronics exports and Asian data-center investment. Medium-term confirmation still requires specific truce, export-control and purchase provisions rather than constructive rhetoric.

The third chain combines foreign cash buying in Taiwan, fewer futures shorts and a sharp rise in local margin financing. The first two support the index while the third increases drawdown sensitivity. Broader participation would allow leverage to reinforce the trend; continued OTC weakness would instead concentrate risk in less-liquid shares.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. Taiwan opens higher but the Taipei Exchange, advancing shares and smaller electronics companies fail to participate.
2. Margin financing continues to expand rapidly after a roughly TWD 9.0 billion daily increase while foreign cash buying fades or reverses.
3. The US 10-year yield returns above 5% and Brent moves back above USD 105, compressing high-multiple valuations.
4. Pre-summit disagreement over technology controls or the trade truce weakens the yuan, Asian chip stocks and freight prices together.
5. Taiwan's export-order headline remains high while industry and geographic concentration rises, leading markets to overstate demand breadth.

## What the Market May Be Missing

**Taiwan's main short-term variable has shifted from whether foreigners will buy to whether foreign buying can outrun the increase in local leverage.** Foreign investors bought TWD 20.38 billion in cash equities and covered 2,029 futures shorts, but margin financing increased by about TWD 9.00 billion while the Taipei Exchange failed to follow the Taiex. If foreign demand slows, holding costs and liquidity risk in smaller shares will appear before weakness in the headline index.

A second underappreciated distinction is that a sharp US semiconductor rally is not the same as a healthy entire market. S&P 500 breadth improved, but Nasdaq new lows still exceeded new highs. If Taiwan also produces strong semiconductor prices but weak OTC breadth, both markets will be pointing to capital concentration rather than a broad demand upgrade.
