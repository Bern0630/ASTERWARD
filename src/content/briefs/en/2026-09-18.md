## Taiwan Pre-Market

### Confirmed Developments

US equities rebounded in the second session after the Federal Reserve's rate increase. The S&P 500 rose 1.14% to 7,637.76, the Dow gained 0.61% to 51,778.04, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 1.69% to 26,418.30, and the Russell 2000 added 0.55% to 2,874.63. **The rebound included both mega-cap technology and smaller companies, but rate-sensitive technology and semiconductors remained the central source of leadership.**

Semiconductor confirmation was strong. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 3.12% to 11,596.49, NVIDIA gained 2.54% to $219.34, and TSMC ADR gained 2.81% to $429.47. The simultaneous gains in TSMC ADR, NVIDIA and the chip index give Taiwan's large electronics names a positive opening impulse. They do not guarantee equal participation from every server, thermal, PCB or smaller electronics supplier.

Cross-asset pressure eased. US Treasury data showed the two-year and ten-year yields falling from roughly 4.74% and 5.01% to 4.67% and 4.94%, while the thirty-year yield declined to 5.29%. The Dollar Index was near 100.22. Brent settled at $104.82 and WTI near $101.91, while VIX fell to 15.44. **Lower oil and long yields directly enabled the valuation rebound, but a 4.67% two-year yield and oil above $100 show that financial conditions have not become genuinely loose.**

Taiwan's previous session gained 439.10 points, or 0.96%, to close at 46,288.00. The index reached 46,874.84 intraday before surrendering more than half of its peak gain, while the OTC index fell 0.26%. Large caps already reflected US semiconductor resilience; participation among smaller companies and aggressive buyers remained unconfirmed.

### Positioning and Flows

Taiwan Stock Exchange data showed foreign investors buying NT$12.185 billion, proprietary dealers buying NT$5.894 billion, dealer hedging accounts selling NT$4.316 billion, and investment trusts buying NT$7.714 billion. The three major institutional groups bought a combined NT$21.477 billion. Foreign cash trading reversed from a NT$17.980 billion sale one day earlier, a one-day swing of roughly NT$30.165 billion that supported TSMC, Hon Hai, Wistron and Quanta.

Derivatives did not turn bullish with cash equities. Foreign investors held 8,161 long and 86,835 short open futures contracts; **foreign investors held a net short of 78,674 Taiwan index futures contracts, 2,323 more net shorts than one day earlier**. The Taiwan index-options volume put/call ratio was 107.24%, while the open-interest ratio was 76.50%. Near-term protection demand was elevated, but outstanding positions were not at an extreme bearish reading. Cash buying and heavier futures hedging show that foreign investors raised stock exposure while retaining macro downside protection.

Listed-market margin balances increased NT$3.229 billion to NT$589.397 billion, while financed shares rose 13,100 lots to 9.2623 million lots. Short-sale balances increased 18,900 lots to 216,300 lots. Securities-lending balances declined by roughly 96 million shares to 25.781 billion shares. Both long and short leverage increased, but lower total securities lending suggests that defense was concentrated in index futures and options rather than broad additional single-stock borrowing.

### Market Pricing

The base case for Taiwan is a stronger open followed by a test of breadth. The US semiconductor and TSMC ADR gains support TSMC, advanced-node manufacturing, advanced packaging, servers and high-speed interconnect. The previous session's large intraday fade, negative OTC close and heavier foreign futures hedge mean that large-cap opening prices alone cannot confirm a broad return of risk appetite.

The US yield curve still signals policy restraint. A 4.67% two-year yield remains high even with the ten-year at 4.94%, and markets priced approximately a 53.1% chance of another quarter-point Fed increase in October. Lower long yields relieve valuation pressure; a high front end continues to constrain unprofitable technology, small caps, real estate and leveraged borrowers.

The Bank of Japan is Asia's main event risk. Markets had almost fully priced a policy-rate increase from 1.00% to 1.25%. The hike itself matters less than Governor Ueda's description of the path toward neutral. A rapid yen appreciation could force the unwinding of yen-funded positions and pressure Asian risk assets. Continued yen weakness would preserve imported-inflation pressure and the case for further tightening.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** Technology and semiconductors retain relative strength, while the index direction depends on the ten-year yield staying below 5%. If Brent remains near or below $105, VIX stays under 17, and credit spreads do not widen, cash-generating mega-cap technology and chip suppliers can extend their rebound. Broad multiple expansion remains constrained by the two-year yield and pricing for another Fed increase.

**Bull case:** The BOJ decision avoids disorderly yen-funded deleveraging, US industrial production is moderate rather than overheated, the ten-year yield falls below 4.90%, and oil extends its decline. Semiconductors, cloud equipment, network interconnect and high-quality growth would lead as investors treat the Fed hike as a restoration of policy credibility.

**Bear case:** BOJ guidance drives a sharp yen rally and global leverage adjustment, or stronger-than-expected US industrial production and capacity utilization lift both two-year and ten-year yields. If oil also returns above $107, the prior session's valuation rebound would unwind first in high-multiple, small-cap and unprofitable technology shares.

### Market Impact

**Fundamental impact:** Moderately positive. NVIDIA, TSMC ADR and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose together, offering no confirmation of weakening AI demand. TSMC's 53.3% year-over-year August revenue growth remains Taiwan's clearest fundamental anchor.

**Valuation impact:** Improved in the near term. A ten-year yield below 5% reduces discount-rate pressure on duration assets, but the high two-year yield favors leaders already producing cash over the entire growth complex.

**Liquidity impact:** Moderately positive but incomplete. Foreign cash buying and US technology gains support the open. Heavier foreign futures shorts, OTC weakness and rising margin financing require stronger breadth confirmation.

**Credit impact:** No immediate deterioration. Lower VIX, long yields and oil reduce near-term funding pressure. Leveraged, floating-rate and long-payback projects still face a long-term cost of capital near 5%.

### Next Confirmation

The first test is whether TSMC can lift turnover and advancing issues after Taiwan opens, with participation from the OTC market, servers, thermal components, PCBs, packaging and power equipment. If TSMC alone lifts the index while the OTC market and most suppliers remain weak, the move is a large-cap rally rather than a broad improvement in risk appetite.

The second test is the joint direction of USD/JPY, Japanese government-bond yields, the Nikkei and Asian currencies after the BOJ decision. The third is US industrial production and capacity utilization, followed by whether the two-year yield, ten-year yield, oil and credit spreads begin rising together again. Malaysia's August inflation and trade releases will show whether high oil first supports exports or is broadening into domestic cost pressure.

## Core Markets Evening Update

### Pre-Market Scorecard

**Confirmed: The positive signal from US semiconductors and TSMC ADR lifted both large caps and smaller electronics companies.** The TAIEX opened higher and closed at its intraday high, gaining 892.75 points, or 1.93%, to 47,180.75 on NT$1.075 trillion of listed-market turnover. TSMC rose 1.44% to NT$2,460, MediaTek gained 4.67%, and ASE advanced 3.91%. The OTC index rose 3.64% to 412.68, decisively repairing the prior session's divergence between strong large caps and weak smaller companies.

Confirmed: Turnover and breadth validated the expansion in risk appetite. There were 759 advancing and 258 declining listed shares, while machinery and construction gained 2.42% and 2.45%. This was not an index move driven only by TSMC. Hon Hai was flat and financials fell 0.96%, however, so leadership remained concentrated in AI, semiconductors and policy-sensitive themes rather than every industry.

Partly confirmed: The morning base case expected the BOJ decision to avoid a disorderly unwind of yen-funded positions. No sharp yen rally or Asian deleveraging occurred, but not because policy turned dovish. Two members dissented from the quarter-point increase to 1.25%, leading markets to question the speed of subsequent hikes. USD/JPY instead moved above 158 as traders focused on the divided vote.

## Taiwan Market

### Confirmed Developments

The TAIEX rose 892.75 points, or 1.93%, to 47,180.75 and closed at the high, while the TPEx index gained 3.64% and advancers outnumbered decliners 759 to 258. **Strength in TSMC, MediaTek, and ASE confirmed that the rebound broadened beyond one index heavyweight into smaller electronics and market breadth.**

### Positioning and Flows

The three major institutional groups bought a combined NT$116.587 billion in the listed market. Foreign investors bought NT$86.994 billion, investment trusts NT$5.289 billion, and proprietary dealer and hedging accounts a combined NT$24.303 billion. **Foreign cash buying accelerated from NT$12.185 billion one day earlier to NT$86.994 billion, confirming the index close at its high, turnover above NT$1 trillion and stronger OTC breadth.**

Futures gave only partial confirmation. Foreign investors held 10,323 long and 86,433 short open Taiwan index-futures contracts, reducing their net short from 78,674 to 76,110 contracts. The volume put/call ratio was 99.35% and the open-interest ratio 76.32%, indicating balanced call and put trading during the session without a stronger stock of downside protection.

Large cash buying, modest futures covering and a low options open-interest ratio are more constructive than the prior session. A 76,110-contract foreign net short nevertheless shows that institutions have not removed macro protection. Final official September 18 margin, short-sale and securities-lending balances were not yet available, so the prior day's figures are not presented as current leverage data.

### Market Pricing

Taiwan advanced from a US-led technology rebound to a locally confirmed cash-flow event. The listed and OTC markets rose together, advancers outnumbered decliners by almost three to one, foreign buying was exceptionally strong, and the index closed at its high. The question is no longer whether a rebound exists, but whether foreign cash buying and trillion-dollar turnover can persist without leverage-driven chasing.

### Market Impact

**Liquidity impact:** Positive with a residual warning. NT$86.994 billion of foreign cash buying, a 3.64% OTC gain and trillion-dollar turnover provide strong confirmation. A 76,110-contract foreign futures net short shows that cash re-entry still carries macro protection.

### Next Confirmation

The first test is whether the US ten-year yield stays below 5% as the cash market absorbs flat industrial production and weaker manufacturing, and whether semiconductors remain firm if yields rise. The second is whether TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and the S&P 500 hold this week's rebound into Friday's close. If chips rise while market breadth deteriorates, the risk-reward of chasing Taiwan higher on Monday worsens.

## Malaysia Market

### Confirmed Developments

Malaysia's August CPI rose 1.9% year over year, up from 1.8% in July; exports increased 45.5%, imports rose 41.1%, and the trade surplus reached MYR 28.1 billion. **External demand and capital-goods activity remained resilient, while higher transport, housing, and food prices showed that energy and household-cost pressure was starting to pass through.**

### Market Pricing

Rapid growth in both exports and imports supports electrical and electronics activity, port logistics, and capital goods, but nominal trade growth should not be read directly as profit growth. Upstream energy and exporters have the stronger buffer; airlines, transport, downstream petrochemicals, consumption, and leveraged long-payback projects remain exposed to cost and financing pressure.

### Market Impact

The fundamental effect is positive while the valuation effect is neutral to negative. Trade momentum and the surplus support the ringgit and corporate revenue, but firmer inflation and elevated global long yields continue to limit valuation expansion in REITs, utilities, data centers, and construction assets.

### Next Confirmation

Watch whether FBM KLCI breadth, the ringgit, and the 10-year Malaysian government bond yield improve together, and whether electrical and electronics, banks, upstream energy, transport, utilities, and data-center shares confirm the trade data through volume and earnings expectations.

## United States Market

### Confirmed Developments

US premarket risk appetite remained positive but restrained. S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures were up roughly 0.1% and 0.3%, with semiconductors firm, while the Stoxx Europe 600 fell about 0.8%. Brent declined toward $103.50 but remained above $100. The US two-year and ten-year Treasury yields rose back toward 4.73% and 4.97%. The post-Fed equity relief rally therefore still has a yield ceiling.

US industrial production was unchanged in August after rising 0.2% in July. Manufacturing output fell 0.3%, ending seven consecutive monthly gains. Total capacity utilization remained at 76.3%, while manufacturing utilization declined 0.3 percentage point to 75.7%. **The report did not add overheating pressure, but weaker manufacturing also limits the case for chasing cyclicals. The next question is whether yields can move away from 5%.**

### Market Pricing

**US premarket trading still combines rising risk assets with a rising risk-free rate.** A third day of lower oil, flat industrial production and weaker manufacturing reduce incremental overheating pressure, but a ten-year yield near 5% and a two-year yield near 4.73% constrain broad multiple expansion. Cash-generating mega-cap technology and semiconductors can outperform as long as yields stay below the week's highs and credit spreads remain stable. Yields rising despite the softer data would show that term premium and policy risk still dominate pricing.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** The S&P 500 and Nasdaq trade with a modest positive bias and semiconductors retain relative strength, while a ten-year yield near 5% limits index upside. Oil below $105 and VIX below 17 would allow investors to treat synchronized central-bank tightening as protection against tail inflation rather than evidence of an imminent demand contraction.

**Bull case:** Markets accept flat industrial production and 76.3% capacity utilization as non-overheating signals, the ten-year yield returns toward 4.90%, and the dollar does not extend its gain. Mega-cap technology, semiconductors, cloud equipment and high-quality growth extend the rebound, supporting Taiwan through ADR prices and continued foreign buying on Monday.

**Bear case:** Despite flat production, policy risk or term premium pushes both two-year and ten-year yields above this week's highs, or renewed Middle East supply risk lifts Brent back above $107. Quarterly derivatives expiration may magnify volume and late-session moves, so one intraday index direction should not be mistaken for a fundamental change.

### Market Impact

**Valuation conditions improved at the margin, but the rate ceiling remained.** Flat industrial production, weaker manufacturing, and lower oil reduced fresh overheating pressure; however, 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields near 4.73% and 4.97% kept high-multiple, unprofitable, and leveraged assets most exposed.

### Next Confirmation

The first test is whether the US ten-year yield stays below 5% as the cash market absorbs flat industrial production and weaker manufacturing, and whether semiconductors remain firm if yields rise. The second is whether TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and the S&P 500 hold this week's rebound into Friday's close. If chips rise while market breadth deteriorates, the risk-reward of chasing Taiwan higher on Monday worsens.

The third test is whether foreign cash buying continues next session and whether the Taiwan index-futures net short falls below 70,000 contracts. If cash buying disappears, futures shorts rise and official margin balances increase, Friday's trillion-dollar turnover may reflect short-term crowding rather than a durable inflow.
## Global Markets and Research

### Japan Market

**Event and Facts:** The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25%, with two members dissenting. The yen weakened to roughly 158.07 after the decision and Japanese equities gained about 1.4%, producing the unusual combination of a rate increase, a weaker currency, and a rising equity market.

**Market Pricing:** Investors cared more about the split vote, the future pace of tightening, and the US-Japan rate gap than the single rate move. Yen weakness implied that markets saw limits on subsequent tightening; banks could benefit from higher rates and exporters from the currency, while high-duration shares remained exposed to rising discount rates.

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** A later hawkish shift could still reprice yen-funded trades and Asian funding costs, affecting offshore positioning in Taiwanese technology and adding volatility to Malaysian currencies and bonds. The next test is whether USD/JPY, Japanese government-bond yields, and the relative performance of banks, exporters, and technology shares confirm the same policy interpretation.

### European Market

**Event and Facts:** Europe's Stoxx 600 traded about 0.8% lower. At the same time, US two-year and ten-year Treasury yields were around 4.73% and 4.97%, while Brent remained above $100 a barrel, leaving Europe exposed to both global rates and energy costs.

**Market Pricing:** Long-duration equities, utilities, and leveraged companies were more sensitive to yields, while airlines, chemicals, and energy-intensive industries also had to absorb input costs. Energy producers and some banks could retain relative support, so the index decline reflected a redistribution of earnings and financing conditions rather than one isolated risk event.

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** European growth and credit conditions affect Taiwanese industrial and technology orders, while energy, trade, and global bond yields transmit to Malaysia. The next confirmation should come from Stoxx 600 breadth, banks and industrials, the euro, European sovereign yields and credit spreads, and whether oil and yields continue to rise together.
## Cross-Market Transmission

The first chain is lower oil, lower inflation risk premium, a ten-year yield below 5%, a technology valuation rebound, and gains in TSMC ADR and NVIDIA that support Taiwan's large electronics companies. For that chain to persist, credit spreads must remain contained and Taiwan's market breadth must improve.

The second chain is simultaneous restraint from the Fed and BOJ. A high US front end keeps dollar funding expensive, while BOJ tightening can alter yen-funded carry trades. Taiwan's high-multiple technology market is exposed to both the dollar return earned by foreign investors and the leverage embedded in regional funding markets.

The third chain separates AI orders from project finance. Strong chip and platform revenue can coexist with a high cost of capital. Large semiconductor leaders may rally before data-center, equipment and construction projects prove cash conversion, inventory discipline, secured power and acceptable payback periods.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. Taiwan opens higher but repeats the prior session's pattern of large-cap strength, weak smaller companies and a fading close.
2. Foreign cash buying is not accompanied by lower Taiwan index-futures shorts, extending the divergence between price and hedging.
3. BOJ tightening or guidance produces a rapid yen rally and carry-trade deleveraging.
4. The US ten-year yield returns above 5% while oil and the dollar rise with it.
5. Margin financing rises into weaker breadth, making local leverage an amplifier during a pullback.

## What the Market May Be Missing

**Large foreign cash buying and a persistent futures net short are not contradictory; together they describe added stock exposure with macro protection still in place.** If cash buying broadens, futures shorts decline and margin financing does not surge, that structure can support a more durable advance. If cash demand fades, futures shorts rise and domestic leverage chases the move, risk will migrate from macro hedging into sharper single-stock and small-cap volatility.

The second underappreciated signal is that the yen stayed weak after the BOJ increased rates. That does not mean policy is ineffective; it means the US-Japan yield gap and the expected pace of future tightening still dominate. A sudden yen rally accompanied by higher Japanese long yields could unwind regional funding trades and pressure high-valuation Asian technology shares faster than a US-rate-only framework would imply.
