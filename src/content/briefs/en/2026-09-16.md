## Taiwan Pre-Market

### Confirmed Developments

US equities closed lower on September 15 as oil and Treasury yields rose together. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell approximately 0.6% to 52,093.11, the S&P 500 lost approximately 0.5% to 7,585.73, and the Nasdaq Composite declined approximately 0.8% to 25,981.57. VIX closed at 17.20, indicating greater demand for protection without disorderly risk aversion.

Rates were the principal source of pressure. The two-, ten- and thirty-year Treasury yields ended the prior session near 4.671%, 5.002% and 5.367%, respectively. The ten-year reached 5.041% intraday, its highest level since 2007. During Asian trading, the ten-year eased to approximately 4.9875% and the Dollar Index traded near 99.675. This was temporary relief, not the removal of a high discount rate.

The energy shock remained active. WTI rose 4.5% in the prior session to approximately $105.84. During Asian trading, Brent fell about 0.8% to $107.86. The decline paused the rally but did not remove supply risk. When oil and Treasury yields rise together, markets are absorbing an inflation risk premium, not merely volatility in one commodity.

### Market Pricing

Ahead of the Fed decision, futures implied an approximately 92.4% probability of a 25-basis-point Fed hike, which would raise the target range from 3.50%-3.75% to 3.75%-4.00%. **A quarter-point move was therefore no longer the primary surprise**. The price-sensitive information would be the economic projections, dot plot and whether the Chair indicated that further tightening could be required in October or December.

The transmission chain is: energy lifts inflation and the expected policy path; the two-year yield reflects hike expectations; the ten-year adds term and fiscal risk premiums; and the dollar transmits tighter conditions into Asia. If credit spreads remain stable, the first effect is valuation compression. If high-yield spreads also widen, the shock moves into financing, capital spending and employment.

AI and semiconductor demand had not shown a confirmed fundamental reversal. NVIDIA gained modestly in the prior session, while TSMC's August revenue rose 53.3% year on year. A yield near 5% nevertheless forces investors to distinguish chip and server suppliers that convert demand into cash quickly from projects dependent on distant utilization, repeated refinancing or aggressive terminal-value assumptions.

### Market Impact

**Fundamental impact:** Neutral. Consumption, employment and the AI supply chain have not confirmed a recession, although high energy costs will reduce household purchasing power and corporate margins.

**Valuation and discount-rate impact:** Clearly negative. A ten-year yield near 5% requires lower valuations or higher cash flow from long-duration equities, real estate and long-payback infrastructure.

**Liquidity and fund-flow impact:** Neutral to negative. A dollar near a two-week high pressures Asian currencies and offshore positions. VIX below 20 and stable US futures do not indicate broad deleveraging.

**Credit impact:** Not yet systemic. Higher risk-free rates first pressure floating-rate, short-maturity and pre-cash-flow borrowers. Whether credit spreads follow is the next-stage test.

### Next Confirmation

Watch the Fed decision and dot plot, the Chair's press conference, and the relative movement of two- and ten-year yields after the announcement. A ten-year yield near 5% with stable credit would remain principally a valuation adjustment. A synchronized rise in rates, the dollar, oil and high-yield spreads would move the risk into financing and earnings.

## Taiwan Post-Market and US Pre-Market

### Pre-Market Scorecard

**Confirmed: high rates and oil compressed valuations without producing a broad breakdown in the AI supply chain.** TSMC, MediaTek and Largan supported Taiwan equities, while a US ten-year yield near 5% continued to cap multiple expansion.

**Partly confirmed: price action was stronger than positioning.** The Taiex gained 0.74%, but foreign investors and the three major institutional groups remained net sellers while domestic margin financing increased. The rebound therefore lacked full cross-market flow confirmation.

**Still to be tested: the policy shock remained ahead.** The Fed decision, dot plot and press conference would determine whether front-end tightening expectations transmitted into long yields, the dollar and credit spreads.

### Confirmed Developments

**US pre-market data**

August US retail and food services sales rose 1.2% month on month and 6.0% year on year to .9 billion. Import prices rose 0.7% on the month and 7.0% on the year, while export prices increased 0.6% and 8.6%, respectively. Firm demand and imported price pressure arrived together, leaving the Fed with resilient growth and renewed inflation costs rather than a sudden demand break.

After the releases, attention centered on the Fed decision, economic projections and the Chair's press conference. Strong retail data raised the bar for dovish guidance. Even with a hike matching expectations, the dot plot and the Chair's description of the October and December path would dominate the two-year yield and the dollar.

**Taiwan**

The Taiex gained 337.41 points to 45,848.90, an increase of 0.74%, with turnover of approximately NT$643.765 billion. The index reached 46,077.82 intraday. TSMC went ex-dividend by NT$7, filled the gap during the session and closed unchanged at NT$2,380. MediaTek and Largan both gained more than 2%, closing at NT$4,530 and NT$6,225, respectively.

The fundamental anchor remained TSMC's August revenue of NT$514.806 billion, up 10.1% month on month and 53.3% year on year. This supports advanced-node and AI-chip demand. The index's fade from its intraday high nevertheless showed that investors were unwilling to expand valuations broadly before the Fed decision.

A firm dollar and a US long yield near 5% remained the main constraints on foreign-investor returns and valuation multiples. The index rose while institutions sold, indicating that large-cap resilience and domestic buying, rather than a broad return of foreign risk appetite, carried the session.

**Malaysia**

Bursa Malaysia was closed for Malaysia Day, so there was no new FBM KLCI cash-market price on September 16. In the prior session, the index fell 18.80 points, or 1.11%, to 1,679.21. Decliners outnumbered advancers by 780 to 355, while turnover was approximately 3.34 billion shares worth RM3.25 billion. Energy was one of the few stronger groups, while utilities, consumer names and selected E&E stocks were under pressure.

Bank Negara Malaysia held the OPR at 2.75% on September 3. As of September 15, the ten-year Malaysian government securities yield was approximately 4.17%, and the Kuala Lumpur USD/MYR reference rate was 4.0795. Official data showed second-quarter GDP growth of 6.0%, first-half growth of 5.7%, and July inflation of 1.8%. Domestic fundamentals therefore remained resilient, while the main pressure came from external funding costs and energy prices.

### Positioning and Flows

TWSE post-close data show foreign investors net sold NT$17.980 billion. Dealers sold NT$3.625 billion in proprietary books and NT$9.349 billion in hedging books, while investment trusts bought NT$10.646 billion. The three institutional groups together sold NT$20.309 billion. **A 0.74% index gain against net institutional selling means the positioning signal was weaker than the price signal.**

At the stock level, foreign investors sold 8.744 million TSMC shares, 9.687 million Wistron, 2.769 million Wiwynn, 1.688 million Hon Hai and 1.302 million Unimicron. They bought 2.606 million ASE Technology shares and 0.997 million MediaTek. This was rotation within the AI supply chain rather than a uniform exit: exposure was reduced in foundry, server assembly and selected high-priced names while packaging and IC design retained sponsorship.

In index futures, foreign investors held 7,805 long and 84,156 short open contracts, for a net short position of 76,351 contracts. September 16 was the monthly settlement date, so rollover can distort the daily change; the absolute net short is best read as hedge intensity, not a standalone market forecast. The Taiwan index-options volume put/call ratio was 106.36%, while the open-interest ratio was 83.56%. Trading demand for downside protection increased, but the stock of options positions was not at an extreme bearish reading.

In leveraged cash positioning, listed-market margin financing rose from NT$582.240 billion to NT$586.167 billion, an increase of NT$3.926 billion. Financed share units rose from 9.191 million lots to 9.249 million lots. Rising prices, foreign selling and expanding margin balances show domestic leverage absorbing supply before a major event. If prices weaken while margin balances remain elevated, forced selling becomes a larger risk than foreign cash selling alone suggests.

### Market Pricing

**Taiwan**

Taiwan is separating earnings growth from multiple compression. TSMC, AI servers and advanced packaging support the fundamentals of TSMC, Quanta, Hon Hai, Wistron, Wiwynn, ASE, MediaTek and Delta, as well as PCB, thermal and power-equipment suppliers. A sustained US long yield above 5%, however, would lower acceptable multiples first for companies whose earnings lie further in the future.

If NVIDIA, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and TSMC ADR remain stable after the Fed while Taiwan monthly revenue and orders do not deteriorate, a pullback should be classified as valuation and fund-flow pressure. If hyperscaler capex guidance, server shipments and supply-chain monthly revenue weaken together, the problem becomes fundamental.

**Malaysia**

The holiday deferred Fed event risk into the September 17 reopening. Banks face a stable OPR and credit growth alongside government-bond valuation risk and higher offshore funding costs. Upstream oil and gas and selected plantations receive commodity support, while airlines, transport, downstream petrochemicals and consumers face higher costs.

E&E exports remain supported by global technology spending, but a US long yield near 5% compresses valuations for semiconductor and technology manufacturers. Construction, utilities, data centers, power equipment and industrial property retain order visibility. REITs and leveraged projects must be repriced against the ten-year government yield, the ringgit and refinancing conditions.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** The Fed raises rates by 25 basis points without committing to consecutive hikes. Strong consumption and price data limit the room for dovish guidance, and equities trade unevenly and the close depends on the ten-year yield, with medium confidence. If the ten-year remains between 4.95% and 5.05%, VIX stays below 19, and Brent does not break $110, cash-generating mega-cap technology, semiconductors and energy retain support. Unprofitable technology, REITs, small caps and leveraged consumer exposures remain weak.

**Bull case:** The Fed maintains restrained forward guidance despite strong consumption and price data, the ten-year falls below 4.95%, the dollar weakens and credit spreads remain contained. The Nasdaq and semiconductors would lead as markets interpret the move as a restoration of policy credibility rather than the start of consecutive tightening.

**Bear case:** The dot plot raises the year-end policy rate, or the Chair treats strong consumption and import prices as grounds for consecutive tightening. A ten-year yield above 5.05%, Brent above $110, and simultaneous gains in the dollar and VIX would spread valuation pressure into credit-sensitive assets.

The base case is invalidated not by a single equity decline but by a simultaneous rise in long yields, the dollar, oil and high-yield spreads. If only the front end rises while long yields and credit remain stable, the market is still primarily repricing the policy path.

### Market Impact

**Taiwan**

**Fundamental impact:** Neutral to positive. TSMC's strong August revenue growth does not support a conclusion that AI demand has broken.

**Valuation and discount-rate impact:** Negative. A US ten-year yield near 5% weighs most heavily on high-multiple, long-payback and capital-intensive companies.

**Liquidity and fund-flow impact:** Negative. Institutions sold a combined NT$20.309 billion, foreign investors carried 76,351 net short futures contracts, and margin financing increased NT$3.926 billion. Price action was stronger than positioning; confirmation requires cash and futures flows to improve together.

**Credit impact:** Limited for large leaders. Smaller server, thermal, PCB and power-equipment suppliers financing expansion with short-dated or floating-rate debt require closer scrutiny of interest coverage and inventory turnover.

**Malaysia**

**Fundamental impact:** Neutral to positive. Second-quarter GDP growth of 6.0%, domestic demand and E&E exports provide support. High oil benefits upstream companies while raising costs for downstream businesses and households.

**Valuation and discount-rate impact:** Negative. External rates and a 4.17% ten-year government yield raise required cap rates for REITs, utilities, data centers and construction assets.

**Liquidity and fund-flow impact:** Negative. The ringgit and foreign flows require confirmation after the reopening, while weak pre-holiday breadth showed that investors had already reduced exposure.

**Credit impact:** Concentrated in long-payback and leveraged projects. Data centers with power, pre-leasing, long contracts and fixed-rate funding are more resilient. Projects with announced capacity but no energization or financing are most exposed.

### Next Confirmation

**Taiwan**

Watch whether TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, NVIDIA and the US ten-year yield confirm the same direction after the Fed. Then test whether foreign cash buying returns, futures net shorts decline and the increase in margin financing slows. Monthly revenue, server shipments, advanced-packaging capacity and hyperscaler capex guidance will distinguish multiple compression from an earnings downgrade.

**Malaysia**

The first test is the joint direction of the FBM KLCI, market breadth, the ringgit and the ten-year government yield when trading resumes on September 17. The second is relative performance across banks, oil and gas, plantations, E&E, utilities, construction, data centers, property and REITs. August inflation and trade data on September 18 will test the energy shock and export resilience.

## Other Key Markets

Asian equities lacked a common direction before the Fed. The MSCI regional gauge was approximately 0.1% higher in the morning, Japan's TOPIX gained as much as approximately 0.8%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng and the Shanghai Composite were each about 0.3% lower. Slightly lower oil supported bonds, but a US long yield near 5% prevented a broad improvement in risk appetite.

The Bank of England and Bank of Japan were scheduled to decide policy over the following two days. If global central banks jointly emphasize energy inflation and financial conditions, the yield shock could spread from the US into Europe and Japan. If they remain patient, the dollar and Treasuries will continue to dominate Asian fund flows.

## Cross-Market Transmission

The first chain is: oil above $100 raises inflation risk and Fed hike expectations; the two-year yield rises; the ten-year adds a term premium; the dollar strengthens; and Asian currencies and foreign-investor returns weaken. Taiwan absorbs the shock through technology valuations. Malaysia absorbs it through the ringgit, government bonds and long-duration infrastructure.

The second chain is: AI end demand supports orders for chips, servers and power equipment, but higher discount rates raise the required return on capex. Equity markets may continue to reward revenue growth. Credit markets require pre-leasing, power access, parent guarantees, debt-service coverage and manageable maturities.

The third chain is: if the Fed lifts only the front end while credit remains stable, the adjustment is valuation and sector rotation. If long yields, the dollar, oil and credit spreads rise together, tighter financial conditions will feed back into consumption, capex and Asian exports.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. The Fed's path beyond the meeting is more hawkish than the already priced quarter-point hike.
2. The US ten-year yield holds above 5.05% while the dollar and oil rise with it.
3. High-yield spreads widen, turning a valuation correction into credit and refinancing stress.
4. Taiwan's index gain fails to receive confirmation from foreign flows, the OTC market and supply-chain revenue breadth.
5. Malaysia's reopening absorbs Fed, currency and energy risk at once, pressuring long-duration assets.

## What the Market May Be Missing

Markets may be underestimating that no hike could also be bearish. With oil above $100, the ten-year yield at 5%, and more than 90% probability assigned to a hike, an unexpected hold could weaken policy credibility and lift the long-end inflation premium instead of producing a conventional easing rally.

The second blind spot is that strong AI fundamentals and valuation compression can coexist. TSMC revenue growth can support earnings expectations, but it cannot neutralize the funding cost of every project. The next winners need orders as well as cash conversion, power, pre-leasing, low refinancing risk and a verifiable payback period.
