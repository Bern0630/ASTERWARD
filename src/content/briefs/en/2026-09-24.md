The two Asian markets point to weaker risk appetite as US pre-market Treasury yields, oil and technology futures deteriorate together. Tonight's main transmission is discount-rate and energy-inflation pressure, not a sudden reversal in artificial-intelligence demand.

Financing value increased while margin shorts and lending shorts declined, leaving leveraged longs exposed without removing institutional hedging.

## Morning Market Outlook

### Transmission Thesis

Strong US activity transmitted to Taiwan through yields and valuations, creating a combination of firm demand and near-term discount-rate pressure. Malaysia's weaker equities and currency offered no additional regional cushion. Taiwan should not simply copy the US decline; the first question is whether overnight futures support extends to TSMC, the OTC market and cash-market breadth.

### Previous US Signals

The September US composite activity reading rose to 58.4, prompting markets to reprice economic heat and the probability of another rate increase. The S&P 500 fell about 0.75% to 7,706, the Nasdaq dropped about 1.13% to 26,936 and the Dow declined about 0.68% to 51,512. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 4.96% to **5.11%**, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 1.41% to 12,510.92. Most sectors outside energy fell, showing pressure from rates and oil rather than one company. **What this means for Taiwan: artificial-intelligence demand was not rejected, but expensive electronics must absorb a higher discount rate. Holding support after the open matters more than the opening gap.**

### Previous Malaysia Signals

The FBM KLCI fell 7.07 points, or 0.42%, to 1,676.43 after touching 1,675.15. Decliners led advancers 549 to 519, volume fell to 3.20 billion shares and value decreased to MYR 2.99 billion. Bank Negara Malaysia's official middle rate was 4.078 ringgit per US dollar, while September index futures closed at 1,665, below cash. Energy and petrochemicals faced oil volatility and profit-taking. **What this means for Taiwan: the region did not confirm a broad risk recovery. Any Taiwan strength is more likely to remain concentrated in electronics leaders with visible orders than to become a market-wide advance.**

### Taiwan Fundamentals and Technicals

The Taiex gained 357.12 points to 48,157.29 on turnover of about TWD 894.65 billion, but only 388 listed stocks advanced while 562 declined. The record index and weak breadth showed that large electronics continued to support the benchmark without broad follow-through. August export orders were still up 71.4% from a year earlier, preserving the electronics-demand case; today's risk is valuation compression rather than a break in fundamentals.

Taiwan index futures fell as low as 47,876 during the overnight session and then recovered to close at 48,497. **The recovery identifies 47,876 through the 48,157 cash close as the first support area, but it becomes a valid turn only if TSMC, the OTC market and advancing issues improve together.** The first upside reference is the overnight high at 48,647. A rising index with persistently negative breadth would remain a concentrated high-level market.

The session must also distinguish large-cap defense from deleveraging in smaller shares. If the Taiex holds while the OTC market breaks its prior low, foreign support for large electronics has not improved domestic risk appetite. If both strengthen, fundamental support is beginning to spread from index leaders into the supply chain.

### Taiwan Positioning

The three institutional groups bought a combined TWD 38.38 billion. Foreign investors bought TWD 37.31 billion, investment trusts sold TWD 4.76 billion and dealers bought TWD 5.82 billion. Foreign investors held 10,855 long and 86,939 short Taiwan index-futures contracts, leaving a net short of 76,084. The options volume put/call ratio was 95.84%, and the open-interest ratio was 79.83%. **Foreign cash support remained strong, but futures hedging was still present, so the cash total alone cannot confirm a breakout.**

Margin-financed positions rose to 9.2823 million lots and TWD 606.37 billion. Margin shorts fell to 213,059 lots, while the securities-lending short-sale balance declined to 32.34 billion shares. Rising financing showed that leveraged buyers were still chasing, while lower short balances showed no parallel increase in bearish exposure. Today's confirmation order is cash breadth, whether foreign futures net shorts expand, and then whether margin financing continues to rise.

### Taiwan Session Outlook

The base case is high-level consolidation between 47,876 and 48,647. If the index holds 48,157, TSMC and the OTC market strengthen together, advancers exceed decliners and foreign futures net shorts stop increasing, the US rate shock can be treated as valuation turnover. Taiwan would then retain the conditions to retest 48,647.

A break below 47,876 with higher turnover, weaker breadth and a larger foreign net short would show that the US 10-year yield above 5% is compressing Taiwan's expensive electronics. Attention would then shift to defending 47,800. Malaysia would become a positive regional input only if the KLCI recovers 1,683 and the ringgit strengthens.

### Invalidation Conditions

The consolidation view fails if Taiwan breaks 47,800 on higher turnover while foreign Taiwan index-futures net shorts exceed 76,084 and listed-market breadth keeps deteriorating. The cautious discount-rate premise fails if the US 10-year yield returns below 5% and the semiconductor index recovers 12,690, allowing Taiwan to reassess a breakout.

## Evening Market Outlook

### Pre-Market Scorecard

| Morning call | Closing result | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consolidation between 47,876 and 48,647 | Closed at 48,024.60, inside the range | Confirmed |
| Holding 48,157 with better breadth was required for strength | Closed below 48,157; 386 listed advancers and 546 decliners | Failed |
| Foreign Taiwan index-futures net short would stop increasing | Net short increased by 947 to 77,031 | Failed |

**The morning range call held, but the expected broadening of support did not occur.** The index stayed above 47,876, so this was not full-scale risk reduction. A close below 48,157, weaker breadth and deeper foreign hedging instead classify the result as deterioration inside the range, not consolidation before a breakout.

### Taiwan Closing Signal

The Taiex lost 132.69 points, or 0.28%, to 48,024.60 on total turnover of about TWD 775.59 billion. TSMC fell TWD 25 to 2,475 and the semiconductor sector lost 0.66%, while computer peripherals and electronic components gained 0.71% and 1.15%, respectively. The modest index decline concealed a rotation away from semiconductor leaders, with 546 listed decliners against 386 advancers. **This is not a collapse in demand. It is a reduction in expensive large-cap exposure under a higher discount rate.**

### Taiwan Positioning Confirmation

Foreign investors sold TWD 32.96 billion and investment trusts sold TWD 12.82 billion. Dealers' proprietary and hedging books together bought TWD 1.34 billion, leaving the three institutional groups with total selling of TWD 44.45 billion. Foreign investors held 9,675 long and 86,706 short Taiwan index-futures contracts. Because long positions declined more, the net short increased by 947 to **77,031**. The options volume put/call ratio rose to 121.11%, while the open-interest ratio was 85.33%, showing greater demand for near-term protection.

Margin-financed positions edged down to 9.2797 million lots, but their value rose to TWD 615.10 billion. Margin shorts declined to 202,008 lots and the securities-lending short-sale balance fell to 31.93 billion shares. **Lower short balances are not an unqualified bullish signal when foreign cash selling, a larger futures net short and higher financing value occur together. The cleaner reading is that shorts did not chase the decline, while leveraged longs assumed more price risk.**

### Malaysia Closing Signal

The FBM KLCI turned positive at midday but ended 4.12 points, or 0.24%, lower at 1,672.31. Decliners led advancers 677 to 436 on 3.32 billion shares worth MYR 2.74 billion. Bank Negara Malaysia's official middle rate weakened from 4.078 to 4.082 ringgit per US dollar, while September index futures closed at 1,664, below cash. Higher oil supported selected energy shares but did not offset pressure from technology, yields and the currency. **For the United States, this confirms an environment in which energy inflation helps producers while compressing broader equity valuations.**

### US Pre-Market Conditions

Before the US open, S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Dow futures were down about 0.6%, 1.0% and 0.3%, respectively. Technology's underperformance matches the weak semiconductor leadership in Taiwan and weaker Malaysian technology shares. The 10-year Treasury yield was around 5.13% and the Dollar Index near 101.29. WTI traded around USD 93.26, Brent near USD 104.78 and the VIX near 16.41 with volatility rising. **Equities, bonds and oil are not pricing a recession. They are pricing resilient demand, renewed energy inflation and policy rates that may remain high for longer.**

The sector implications are uneven. Energy producers receive revenue support from oil and banks can benefit from a higher or steeper rate structure. Large technology, semiconductors and other long-duration assets face valuation compression first. Taiwan's semiconductor weakness alongside gains in selected electronics components did not invalidate AI supply-chain demand. It does imply that a US rebound led only by energy, with weak technology and breadth, would remain low quality.

After the open, confirmation should be read in three stages: first, whether the 10-year yield retreats; second, whether the Nasdaq's underperformance against the Dow narrows; and third, whether advancing issues move above half the market. A rally confined to a few technology leaders while the yield remains above 5.10% would be large-cap covering. A lower yield, semiconductor leadership and positive breadth together would indicate genuine relief from discount-rate pressure. If oil rises while yields refuse to fall, energy leadership is an inflation warning rather than evidence of better economy-wide earnings.

### US Session Outlook

**Base case, about 55%: a lower open followed by consolidation, with technology lagging and energy relatively resilient.** If the 10-year yield remains between 5.10% and 5.15% and Brent holds between USD 104 and USD 106, any S&P 500 intraday recovery is more likely to reflect position adjustment than relief from valuation pressure. Confirmation would be a persistently larger Nasdaq decline than the Dow, fewer than half of issues advancing and semiconductors failing to lead a futures recovery.

**Downside case, about 30%: another rise in yields and oil triggers a second round of technology deleveraging.** If the 10-year yield breaks 5.15%, Brent moves above USD 106 and the Nasdaq 100 opens more than 1.5% lower, investors would translate the energy shock into higher inflation and tighter policy for longer. Pressure would then spread from semiconductors to software, consumer discretionary stocks and small caps.

**Upside case, about 15%: yields retreat first and technology recovers most of its pre-market loss.** If the 10-year yield falls below 5.05%, oil gives back its gains and the Nasdaq futures decline narrows to less than 0.3%, Taiwan's supply-chain rotation can again be read as evidence that demand remains intact. Only then would the S&P 500 have the conditions to progress from a technical bounce into a stable recovery. Tonight's key test is not simply whether indexes finish green or red, but whether yields, technology relative strength and breadth improve together.

### Invalidation Conditions

The rates-and-oil valuation-pressure thesis fails if the 10-year yield falls below 5.05%, Brent drops below USD 103 and the Nasdaq turns positive. Conversely, a yield above 5.15% with a widening technology decline invalidates the consolidation base case and activates the downside case.
