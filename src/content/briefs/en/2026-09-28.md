## Evening Market Outlook

### Taiwan Market-Closed State

The Taiwan Stock Exchange is closed for Teachers' Day. There are no current readings for the Taiex, market breadth, institutional cash flows, foreign Taiwan index futures, options, margin balances or securities lending. The next trading day is September 29. Defensive foreign cash and futures positioning from the previous session remains background information only and cannot be presented as today's confirmation.

The nearby SGX FTSE Taiwan Index futures contract provides an offshore proxy while Taiwan is closed. On September 28 it traded at 4,258.00, down 0.33%, within a 4,250.50 to 4,288.50 range, on volume of about 21,540 contracts. The offshore market therefore did react, but its constituents, venue and positioning differ from the Taiwan cash market. **It can indicate direction, but it cannot replace cash-market breadth or institutional positioning.**

**What this means for the United States:** The modest decline in FTSE Taiwan futures aligns with weakness in Malaysian technology and US semiconductor shares before the open, so the regional technology signal is not merely a data gap caused by Taiwan's closure. The move remains limited, and the next full supply-chain test will be the opening performance of Taiwan's electronics heavyweights, breadth and foreign flows when trading resumes.

### Malaysia Closing Signal

The FBM KLCI closed at 1,670.02, down 1.60 points or 0.09%, after trading between 1,668.61 and 1,674.11. The headline move was small, but 748 stocks declined against 388 advancers. Turnover fell to 3.11 billion shares worth MYR 2.40 billion from 4.07 billion shares and MYR 2.69 billion in the previous session. The FBM 70 fell 0.88%, the FBM Emas declined 0.34%, and technology-related Inari lost MYR 0.11 to MYR 2.55. Select financial heavyweights cushioned the index while selling was substantially broader underneath.

**What this means for the United States:** Malaysia is not a direct driver of tonight's US market, but it provides a useful cross-check. As energy and rate pressure rose, Asian investors reduced exposure to technology and mid-caps first, matching the direction of US semiconductor weakness before the open. This raises the probability of weak Nasdaq breadth, but it cannot determine the magnitude of a US decline by itself.

### US Pre-Market Conditions

Major US exchanges are scheduled to open normally. Current sources place Dow futures about 0.35% to 0.42% lower, S&P 500 futures about 0.38% to 0.50% lower, and Nasdaq 100 futures about 0.70% to 1.00% lower. Growth is clearly underperforming the broader market. The same pattern appears in individual pre-market moves: Nvidia was down about 0.4%, Broadcom 1.2%, Micron 2.2%, AMD 2.7% and Intel 3.3%.

The main constraint is not a company-specific headline but the discount rate. The US 10-year Treasury yield is around 5.21%, near a multi-year high. The Dollar Index is near 101.10 and slightly firmer, while oil remains higher. **Oil raises the risk that inflation and policy rates stay elevated, and long yields transmit that pressure directly into high-valuation technology shares. The two forces are tightening in the same direction.** Reliable sources disagree on exact crude prices, so direction rather than an inconsistent point estimate is used as the threshold. A dependable live pre-market VIX reading was also unavailable, making post-open volatility and breadth necessary confirmation signals.

### US Session Outlook

**The baseline is a lower open followed by a weak and volatile session, with Nasdaq and semiconductors lagging and downside breadth worse than the headline indices imply.** Taiwan's cash market is closed, but the 0.33% decline in FTSE Taiwan futures provides limited negative offshore confirmation, while Malaysia's negative breadth and technology weakness corroborate the pre-market setup. If the 10-year yield holds around 5.20% or higher, Nasdaq continues to lag the S&P 500 and semiconductor losses fail to narrow materially, valuation pressure is likely to persist into the cash session.

The first secondary path is relative energy strength, but that would not constitute a broad risk-on turn. Higher oil can support energy-sector cash flow while raising costs and discount rates elsewhere. If energy advances while technology, small caps and breadth remain weak, the move should be read as an inflation trade rather than bullish participation. The second path is a rate reversal. A 10-year yield below 5.15%, a Nasdaq recovery toward flat and semiconductor leadership in narrowing losses would be sufficient to move the baseline from defensive to neutral.

**Suggested exposure:** Defensive. Do not chase an early index rebound before long yields and semiconductors stabilize together. Move toward neutral only when yields fall and breadth improves.

**Sector tilt:** Prefer defensive and quality companies with stable cash flow and lower rate sensitivity. Keep semiconductors and long-duration growth underweight for observation. Energy may outperform relatively, but that is not a signal to raise broad equity risk.

**Validity horizon:** The September 28 regular US session only. After the close, score the call against the actual index path, semiconductor relative performance, the 10-year yield and market breadth.

### Invalidation Conditions

The discount-rate-led weak-session thesis fails if the 10-year yield falls below 5.15%, Nasdaq moves from clear underperformance toward flat or leadership, semiconductor losses narrow at the same time, and advancers exceed decliners. Conversely, a yield above 5.20%, persistent semiconductor underperformance and widening negative breadth confirm the baseline. Energy gains or relative resilience in the Dow alone do not invalidate it.
