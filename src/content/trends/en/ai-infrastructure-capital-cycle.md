> Data cutoff: September 16, 2026, 15:00 Asia/Taipei. This report establishes a capital-cycle framework and does not treat announced investment as delivered supply or realized demand.

## What Is Changing

AI investment has expanded from a product cycle concentrated in a few chip and cloud companies into a capital cycle spanning wafers, advanced packaging, servers, networks, cooling, power, land, data centers and credit markets. The early question was whether GPUs were scarce. The current questions are when new supply arrives, who funds it, whether utilization rises, and whether capital returns exceed funding costs.

TSMC's August revenue reached NT$514.806 billion, up 53.3% year on year, showing that chip demand remained strong. At the same time, the US ten-year Treasury yield approached 5% while the capital required for data centers and grids increased rapidly. Demand growth and capital discipline are now the same investment problem.

## Why Now

Bottlenecks are moving through the chain. As chip supply expands, constraints can migrate into advanced packaging, grid connection, transformers, cooling, networks and construction. Each new bottleneck encourages more investment, but when every segment expands together, markets are also more likely to overestimate future utilization.

High rates accelerate differentiation. Cash-rich hyperscalers can sustain strategic investment, while developers dependent on short-term funding, distant valuations or one tenant face greater refinancing risk. A capital cycle need not end in a demand collapse. It can adjust through lower returns, delayed commissioning and consolidation.

## End Demand

End demand includes model training, inference, search, advertising, enterprise software, coding, automation, government and sovereign compute. Training demand is concentrated and large. Inference can be more persistent, but lower unit prices and efficiency gains can reduce compute required for each task.

Investors should track paid workloads, cloud revenue, utilization, revenue per unit of compute, customer retention and actual deployments rather than only model counts or announced capex. Chip shipments are a leading supply indicator, not proof that end usage has monetized.

## Business Model

Chip companies recover research spending through high-margin products. Foundries and packaging companies recover fixed assets through utilization. Server and component suppliers rely on margin, working-capital velocity and scale. Cloud providers monetize platforms and rented compute. Data centers recover capital through power, space and long leases. GPU rental models depend on equipment utilization and residual value.

The same AI expenditure can appear multiple times as both revenue and capex across the chain. Analysis must avoid adding supplier orders, cloud capex and end demand as though they were separate final uses.

## Who Pays

Payers include hyperscale cloud providers, AI laboratories, enterprises, governments, advertisers and consumers. High-quality demand has verifiable usage, minimum purchases, prepayments, long contracts or parent guarantees. Customers that depend on the next fundraising round to pay cloud bills or GPU rent have materially weaker credit.

## Who Borrows

Borrowers extend from chip manufacturers and server suppliers into data-center developers, utilities, telecommunications companies, REITs, GPU special-purpose vehicles and their suppliers. Debt can sit on corporate balance sheets, project companies, leases, supplier finance or private credit. Public-company net debt alone can understate leverage across the system.

## Capital Structure

A sound structure funds growth through operating cash flow, long-dated fixed-rate debt and phased capex while matching asset life with debt maturity. A weak structure uses short-dated floating-rate money to purchase rapidly depreciating equipment and assumes utilization, rents, valuations and rate cuts improve together.

Leases, purchase commitments, minimum payments, supplier finance and special-purpose vehicles should be recombined to assess true interest coverage and free cash flow.

## Credit Structure

Credit risk begins with utilization below plan and travels through revenue shortfalls, weaker interest coverage, lower equipment residual values and higher refinancing costs. Equities can initially rise on orders while credit weakens on debt terms. That divergence is often an early sign of an overheated capital cycle.

## Bull Case

Paid inference and enterprise adoption grow rapidly, cloud utilization rises, and chip, server and data-center supply arrives on schedule. Long yields fall while credit spreads remain stable. Revenue becomes free cash flow and return on invested capital remains above funding cost.

## Bear Case

The industry continues to build, but model efficiency, customer budgets or competition reduce compute pricing. Power and construction are delayed while funding costs remain high. Orders do not disappear immediately, yet utilization and cash recovery undershoot underwriting, eventually causing capex delays, asset sales and credit losses.

## Weakest Link

The weakest exposures are compute-reselling or data-center projects without long-term customers, reliable power and complete financing, as well as suppliers whose receivables and inventory grow faster than revenue. Financing short-lived GPUs against long-duration real-estate valuations is another clear maturity mismatch.

## Leading Indicators

1. Cloud revenue, paid workloads, utilization and revenue per unit of compute.
2. Hyperscaler capex and free cash flow.
3. TSMC monthly revenue, advanced-packaging utilization, GPU, ASIC and server shipments.
4. Supplier receivables, inventory and cash-conversion cycles.
5. Data-center capacity split into power-secured, financed, pre-leased, energized and utilized stages.
6. US long yields, corporate spreads, private credit and project-finance terms.
7. GPU rental rates, secondary-market prices and equipment residual values.

## What the Market May Be Missing

Strong AI demand and an overheated capital cycle can coexist. Leading companies may sustain high revenue growth while marginal projects already earn lower returns. Asking only whether AI is a bubble misses the more useful distinction: which segments remain scarce, and where new supply is beginning to outrun monetizable demand.

## Next Confirmation

Compare growth in cloud capex, cloud revenue and free cash flow over the next two quarters, then match it against chip and server shipments, actual data-center load and credit conditions. The cycle remains healthy if investment, utilization and cash flow rise together. If capex grows while utilization, free cash flow and financing terms weaken, supply is moving ahead of demand.
