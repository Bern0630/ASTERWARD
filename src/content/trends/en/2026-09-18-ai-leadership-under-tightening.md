## Research Thesis

Artificial-intelligence equities have not lost their structural demand because global central banks resumed tightening. What has changed is the standard for leadership. The Federal Reserve lifted its target range to 3.75%-4.00% on September 16, and the Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25% on September 18. The US ten-year Treasury yield briefly exceeded 5% this week, while Brent approached $110. Capital now asks not only whether AI orders are growing, but when revenue becomes cash, who funds the investment, and whether earnings growth can outrun the discount rate.

This is not a binary AI bull-or-bear call. TSMC's August revenue rose 53.3% from a year earlier. On September 17, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gained 3.12% and TSMC ADR advanced 2.81%. On September 18, TSMC, MediaTek and ASE rose together while Taiwan's OTC index gained 3.64%. Demand and price action do not confirm an industry reversal. **The real change is that the market is assigning different prices to the same unit of growth, concentrating leadership among companies that can prove demand, execution, margins and cash conversion at once.**

## Evidence and Market Pricing

The TAIEX gained 995.90 points, or 2.16%, for the week and ended Friday at 47,180.75. Listed-market turnover returned to NT$1.075 trillion, with 759 advancing and 258 declining shares. The post-Fed response suggests investors initially treated the hike as insurance against tail inflation rather than an immediate threat to demand. Technology and smaller companies also rose together on Friday, rejecting the weakest version of the thesis that one large constituent alone drove the rebound.

Leadership remained selective. TSMC closed at NT$2,460, MediaTek gained 4.67%, and ASE rose 3.91%, but Hon Hai was flat and financials lost 0.96%. US premarket trading showed a similar pattern, with Nasdaq futures outperforming Dow futures, semiconductors firm and Europe's defensive indexes weaker. Capital was not pursuing risk indiscriminately. It favored businesses capable of using growth to offset a higher cost of capital.

## Qualification One: Verifiable Demand

The first threshold is demand that can be verified through revenue, shipments, usage or enforceable contracts. Chip orders, cloud capital expenditure and announced data-center capacity are not identical. They may count the same underlying demand several times along one supply chain. High-quality demand identifies the final payer, minimum-payment obligation, acceptance schedule and the period in which revenue converts into cash.

TSMC monthly revenue, advanced-packaging utilization, server shipments and cloud revenue are closer to realized demand. AI partnerships, distant capacity plans and data-center projects without disclosed tenants remain intentions. When the risk-free rate approaches 5%, markets shorten their tolerance period and become less willing to substitute a distant terminal value for near-term cash flow.

## Qualification Two: Supply Execution

Strong demand does not guarantee that every supplier can deliver. The AI bottleneck has expanded from GPUs to advanced packaging, high-bandwidth memory, power modules, transformers, cooling, optical networking, land and grid interconnection. Companies controlling scarce capacity, sustaining yields and delivering on schedule can turn scarcity into price and margin. Companies with orders but insufficient capacity or working capital may instead develop cash shortfalls during growth.

Revenue must therefore be read together with inventory, receivables, capital expenditure and free cash flow. If sales rise while receivable days, inventory days and short-term borrowing rise faster, a supplier may be financing its customers. If utilization fails to rise after expansion, depreciation and interest can turn order growth into lower shareholder returns.

## Qualification Three: Capital Structure

Global tightening widens the gap between capital structures. Net-cash leaders with stable free cash flow and long-dated fixed-rate debt can maintain research and investment during volatility and take share when weaker competitors retreat. Businesses dependent on short-term floating-rate debt, vendor finance, equipment leases or special-purpose vehicles carry interest-rate, refinancing and residual-value risk at the same time.

BOJ tightening matters because the yen has long been a low-cost global funding currency. USD/JPY rose toward 158 after this hike, so there was no immediate carry unwind, but the policy rate has entered the lower end of the BOJ's estimated neutral range. **A weaker yen today does not mean funding remains permanently loose; it moves the risk from an immediate price shock to future funding costs and leveraged-position adjustments.**

## Transmission Mechanism

Global tightening first changes valuation through discount rates, then changes corporate decisions through debt costs, working capital and capital-spending hurdles. Demand, execution and capital structure therefore form a sequence rather than a checklist: end demand must become deliverable revenue, revenue must become cash, and cash determines whether a company can sustain investment and shareholder returns under a higher cost of capital.

## Bull Case

The constructive path requires AI workloads and cloud revenue to keep growing while chips, packaging, networking and power equipment arrive on time. Lower oil would keep the US ten-year yield below 5%, and credit spreads would remain contained. Large platforms, foundries, scarce-component suppliers and power-infrastructure companies could absorb the discount rate through earnings growth.

Leadership would broaden with discipline. Suppliers showing simultaneous revenue and cash-flow growth would lead, followed by equipment companies with visible orders and controlled capital expenditure, and only later by distant-duration businesses that still lack free cash flow. Taiwan confirmation would include continued foreign cash buying, persistent OTC relative strength and a decline in the foreign index-futures net short from 76,110 contracts.

## Bear Case

The adverse path does not require AI demand to collapse. If renewed oil inflation forces faster Fed and BOJ tightening and US long yields break above this week's highs, customers may first cancel low-priority projects, delay data-center activation or demand longer payment terms. Chip orders can remain strong while downstream project returns and financing capacity deteriorate.

Overbuilding is the second risk. If multiple layers expand on the same high-growth assumptions, any delay or efficiency gain may leave utilization below underwriting models. Equity multiples compress first, credit spreads rise next, and only later do depreciation, inventory and impairment appear in financial statements.

## Leading Indicators

1. Whether monthly revenue, gross margin and free cash flow rise together at TSMC and major suppliers.
2. Whether receivables and inventory persistently grow faster than sales.
3. Whether US two-year and ten-year yields, the dollar, oil and credit spreads rise together.
4. Whether foreign Taiwan cash flows and index-futures hedges converge after their recent divergence.
5. The conversion ratio between cloud revenue, AI-service usage and capital expenditure.
6. Data-center power secured, energized, pre-leased and utilized, rather than headline announced megawatts.
7. Whether later BOJ hikes trigger rapid yen appreciation and a reduction in global carry positions.

## Falsification

Two outcomes would invalidate this trend. First, global yields could fall rapidly without recession, allowing investors to raise valuations broadly across distant growth assets; balance-sheet differences would then matter less. Second, AI end revenue and usage could miss expectations broadly enough that even cash-rich leaders cut capital expenditure. That would be a demand-cycle reversal, not a reordering of leadership.

Until either occurs, the useful question is not whether AI can continue rising. It is where each company sits across demand quality, execution, cash conversion and capital structure. **The next phase of excess return is more likely to come from fundamental separation than from treating every unit of AI exposure as the same trade.**

## Next Confirmation

US August industrial production was unchanged, manufacturing output fell 0.3%, and capacity utilization held at 76.3%. The immediate test is whether the US ten-year yield remains below 5% after the softer report and whether semiconductors retain relative strength through quarterly derivatives expiration. Taiwan's next session will test continued foreign cash buying, OTC turnover and whether the foreign index-futures net short falls below 70,000 contracts.

The medium-term test is third-quarter reporting. AI revenue needs to be accompanied by better margins, operating cash flow and return on invested capital. If orders and capital expenditure rise while cash flow and credit conditions deteriorate, concentrated leadership will persist.
