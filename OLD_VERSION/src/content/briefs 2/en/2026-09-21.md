## Taiwan Pre-Market

### Confirmed Developments

US equities finished Friday with a split tape. The S&P 500 gained 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.39% to 26,522.55, while the Dow slipped 0.18% to 51,682.64 and the Russell 2000 fell 0.49% to 2,860.40. For the week, the S&P 500 edged down 0.1%, the Nasdaq rose 0.7%, the Dow lost 1.7% and the Russell 2000 fell 1.5%. Most US stocks declined even as the headline growth indexes held up.

Semiconductors materially outperformed. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 2.78% to 11,921.69, NVIDIA gained 1.34% to USD 222.27 and TSMC ADRs added about 1.03% to USD 434.67. **The chip signal supports Taiwan's large electronic weights, but the combination of stronger semiconductors, a weaker Russell 2000 and negative market breadth is not evidence of a broad return to risk.**

Rates remain the principal constraint. The US 10-year Treasury yield returned to about 5.00%, the 30-year yield was near 5.34% and Brent crude settled around USD 103.9 a barrel. Strong artificial-intelligence demand can support revenue expectations, but it does not remove the discount-rate, financing-cost and payback-period constraints on valuation.

Risk appetite was mildly positive in early Asian trading on Monday. S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures edged higher and the dollar was little changed, while a Japanese holiday left liquidity thinner with the yen near 157 per dollar. US and Chinese officials are meeting in New York on trade, investment, artificial intelligence and the Middle East. Markets are pricing a chance of lower friction before a leaders summit, not a confirmed policy outcome.

### Positioning and Flows

Taiwan's Taiex rose 892.75 points, or 1.93%, to 47,180.75 in the previous session. There were 759 listed gainers against 258 decliners and total market turnover exceeded TWD 1 trillion. The three institutional groups bought a net TWD 116.59 billion, including TWD 86.99 billion from foreign investors, TWD 5.29 billion from investment trusts and TWD 24.30 billion from dealers. **Price, turnover, breadth and foreign cash buying all confirmed the advance, providing Monday's strongest positive foundation.**

Futures still carried protection. Foreign investors held 10,323 long and 86,433 short Taiex futures contracts, leaving a net short of 76,110 contracts after covering only 2,564 contracts. The Taiex options put/call volume ratio was 99.35%, while the open-interest ratio was 76.32%. Heavy cash buying with only limited futures covering indicates that investors increased stock exposure without removing broad downside hedges.

Listed-market margin financing rose by TWD 4.64 billion to TWD 594.04 billion, while financed units increased by 18,022 lots to 9.28 million. Short-financed units increased by 6,677 lots to 222,965. **Foreign buying and higher domestic leverage can extend momentum, but they also make a post-open loss of breadth a more important warning signal.**

### Market Pricing

The base case for Taiwan is a semiconductor-led higher open followed by a breadth test. TSMC ADRs, NVIDIA and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index all advanced, supporting foundry, advanced packaging, servers and high-speed connectivity. However, a 5% US 10-year yield and weaker US small caps mean that high-valuation themes with distant cash flows need not follow the index leaders.

Malaysia provides a useful Asian comparison. The FBM KLCI fell 0.54% to 1,665.56 on Friday, yet 625 gainers exceeded 564 decliners and turnover rose to MYR 4.87 billion as money rotated into smaller technology and construction shares. The 10-year Malaysian government bond yield fell to 3.92%, while USD/MYR traded between roughly 4.0770 and 4.0970. The divergence shows that weakness in large benchmark weights does not necessarily mean domestic risk appetite has collapsed.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** US-China talks retain a controlled tone and the modest rise in index futures holds, but a 5% 10-year yield caps the broader index. Large chips, platforms and cash-generative technology outperform leveraged growth, leaving market breadth narrow.

**Bull case:** Negotiators provide concrete indications of an extended trade truce, lower export-control uncertainty or additional purchases, while the dollar and long yields stop rising. Broader participation in the S&P 500 would improve order visibility for Taiwan semiconductors and Malaysia's electrical and electronics supply chain.

**Bear case:** The talks produce no executable outcome, or Brent returns above USD 105 and pushes Treasury yields higher. If the Nasdaq continues to rely on a small group of chip leaders while the Russell 2000 and credit-sensitive industries weaken, index gains would conceal increasing concentration risk.

### Market Impact

**Flows:** Heavy foreign cash buying and high turnover support Taiwan, but a 76,110-contract foreign futures net short and higher margin financing mean the rally still depends on continued foreign demand rather than domestic leverage alone.

**Industries:** Semiconductors have the clearest price confirmation, led by foundry, advanced packaging, servers and high-speed connectivity. Power, cooling and data-center infrastructure still require evidence from orders, margins, power delivery and cash conversion.

**FX and rates:** A yen near 157 and a broadly steady dollar preserve order in Asian risk assets, but a US 10-year yield near 5% caps valuation expansion. Lower Malaysian government bond yields and a relatively stable ringgit offer a buffer for local banks, technology and construction.

**Energy and credit:** Brent above USD 100 pressures Taiwan's transport, chemical and power costs. Malaysia's upstream energy sector and fiscal receipts benefit relatively, while airlines, utilities and long-payback data-center projects still face higher operating and funding costs.

### Next Confirmation

Three tests matter after Taiwan opens: whether the Taiex and Taipei Exchange rise together, whether gainers continue to outnumber decliners decisively, and whether semiconductor and server leaders extend without disorderly volume. A higher index with quickly deteriorating breadth should be treated as an extension in benchmark weights rather than a broad bull move.

During the session, watch USD/TWD, Taiex futures basis and the yen. A sharp yen rally in holiday-thinned trading, a firmer dollar and a weakening futures basis would indicate tighter Asian positioning. A healthier continuation requires further foreign cash buying, another decline in futures shorts and no renewed surge in margin financing.

## Taiwan Market

### Confirmed Developments

The Taiex gained 538.09 points, or 1.14%, to 47,718.84 after reaching 47,750.98, closing near the session high. Turnover was TWD 820.22 billion, down from more than TWD 1 trillion in the previous session. TSMC rose 0.81% to TWD 2,480, MediaTek gained 6.37% to a record TWD 5,010, Delta Electronics advanced 8.07% and AUO rose 10%. Hon Hai slipped 0.20% and Nanya Technology fell 1.71%. **MediaTek, Delta, displays and financials joined TSMC in supporting the index, but stock-level divergence remained material.**

The Taipei Exchange edged down 0.03% to 412.57, diverging from the listed market. The breadth test set in the morning was therefore confirmed more fully on the main board than among smaller stocks, as capital concentrated in liquid technology, displays and financials.

### Positioning and Flows

The three institutional groups bought a net TWD 47.04 billion on the main board, including TWD 20.38 billion from foreign investors, TWD 3.93 billion from investment trusts and TWD 22.73 billion from dealers. Foreign buying was materially below the prior session's TWD 86.99 billion but remained positive. Foreign demand was concentrated in MediaTek, AUO, TSMC, Taiwan Union Technology and Delta Electronics; foreign investors bought 235,893 AUO lots, making displays the most visible stock-level flow.

Futures hedging also declined. Foreign investors held 10,318 long and 84,399 short Taiex futures contracts, leaving a net short of 74,081 contracts, 2,029 fewer than in the prior session. The Taiex options put/call volume ratio rose to 125.10%, while the open-interest ratio increased to 96.20%. **Cash buying and futures covering were bullish, but heavier put turnover showed that investors did not remove short-term protection as the index reached a three-month high.**

September 21 main-board margin, short-financing and securities-lending balances have not yet been published. The September 18 margin balance of TWD 594.04 billion is therefore not used as a substitute for today's leverage. Prices and institutional flows are confirmed; whether retail leverage chased the move remains unresolved.

### Market Pricing

Taiwan progressed from a US-chip rebound to continued domestic institutional buying, albeit at a lower intensity than in the prior session. A close near the high, positive foreign cash flow and fewer futures shorts support trend continuation. Lower turnover, a weaker Taipei Exchange and a 125.10% options volume put/call ratio show a stronger preference for large, liquid names.

**The market is pricing durable artificial-intelligence capital spending without granting every electronics company the same risk premium.** MediaTek, Delta and TSMC benefited from platform, order or power-efficiency narratives, while memory and some server assemblers failed to keep pace. That split says more about required earnings quality than the headline index gain.

### Market Impact

Better alignment between cash and futures supports large semiconductors, ASICs, power-management names and financials. Weakness on the Taipei Exchange and foreign selling in over-the-counter shares constrain smaller-stock momentum. With only three full sessions left before the holiday, continued gains require further foreign cash buying, fewer futures shorts and no additional collapse in turnover.

### Next Confirmation

The next session should test whether the Taiex and Taipei Exchange can realign, and whether MediaTek, Delta and TSMC move from momentum buying to stable sponsorship. Another Taiex high accompanied by a weaker Taipei Exchange, smaller foreign buying and a high options put/call ratio would signal concentration rather than breadth expansion.

The second test is the September 21 margin, short-financing and securities-lending release. A large increase in margin financing as turnover contracts would mean that domestic leverage is rising faster than liquidity. Moderate margin use alongside further foreign futures covering would provide a more durable configuration.

## Malaysia Market

### Confirmed Developments

The FBM KLCI gained 1.38 points, or 0.08%, to 1,666.94 after trading between 1,661.22 and 1,674.74. Gainers narrowly exceeded decliners, but volume fell from 4.91 billion to 3.41 billion shares and value declined from MYR 4.87 billion to MYR 2.8 billion. The FBM70 fell 0.87% while the FBM ACE advanced, showing different directions among benchmark large caps, mid-caps and growth-oriented smaller stocks.

Stocks and industries rotated sharply. Hong Leong Bank, Hengyuan Refining and selected solar names advanced, while Malaysian Pacific Industries, Time dotCom, Sunway Construction, Press Metal and Petronas Gas declined. **The small benchmark gain was not a broad rally; financials, refining and selected renewables offset weakness in technology, construction and industrial heavyweights.**

### Positioning and Flows

USD/MYR declined to 4.0755/4.0800 from Friday's 4.0795/4.0840, representing a modest ringgit appreciation alongside lower oil and better regional risk sentiment. The sharp contraction in equity turnover shows that the currency improvement did not generate broad equity chasing.

Complete September 21 daily foreign-flow data have not yet been published, so the slight FBM KLCI gain cannot substitute for foreign-flow confirmation. The observable combination is a firmer ringgit, slightly positive breadth and lower turnover, which is more consistent with selection and short covering than a large return of foreign capital.

### Market Pricing

Malaysia split the oil decline into two paths. Airlines, consumers and some utilities benefit from lower input costs, while upstream energy loses part of its risk premium. A slightly higher KLCI alongside weaker Petronas Gas and stronger Hengyuan shows that investors are distinguishing gas infrastructure, refining margins and crude prices rather than trading energy as one block.

The domestic policy and macro baseline did not change, with the overnight policy rate still at 2.75%. **A firmer ringgit provides a valuation buffer, but turnover of only MYR 2.8 billion means that technology, construction and data-center rebounds still require volume and foreign-flow confirmation.**

### Market Impact

Electrical and electronics companies and data centers retain support from global artificial-intelligence demand, but declines in Malaysian Pacific Industries, Time dotCom and Sunway Construction show that revenue growth does not automatically remove funding, power and construction constraints. Banks benefited from selective blue-chip demand, airlines and consumers gained from lower oil, and upstream energy lost part of its commodity support.

### Next Confirmation

Malaysia's MYR 7 billion reopening of a seven-year government bond on September 22 will test domestic duration demand and whether lower global yields transmit into the sovereign curve. In equities, watch daily foreign flows, whether the KLCI can hold 1,665 with higher turnover, and whether electrical and electronics, banks, energy, construction and data-center-related shares gain together.

## United States Market

### Confirmed Developments

US pre-market risk appetite strengthened materially from the early Asian session. S&P 500 futures rose about 0.7%, Nasdaq 100 futures gained about 1.1% and Dow futures advanced about 0.8%. Brent fell roughly 3% to near USD 101 a barrel, while the US 10-year Treasury yield declined from Friday's 5.00% to about 4.92%. **Equity futures, oil and yields all moved in a direction favorable to growth stocks, exceeding the mildly positive morning base case.**

The tone of preliminary US-China talks also improved. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described trade and artificial-intelligence discussions with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng as very successful, while the United States proposed an AI incident-notification and formal-dialogue framework. Reports of a possible US-Iran leaders meeting at the United Nations reduced the energy supply-risk premium. These are positive catalysts, not final agreements.

### Market Pricing

The clearest pre-market chain is diplomatic optimism in the Middle East, oil approaching USD 101, lower inflation risk premia, a sub-5% 10-year yield and technology-led futures gains. This is more than a single headline response because equities, bonds and energy jointly confirm a short-term easing in financial conditions.

**The market still has to prove that the move can spread from artificial-intelligence leaders to small caps, industrials and credit-sensitive industries.** If the Nasdaq leads after the cash open while the Russell 2000, equal-weight indexes and breadth fail to participate, Friday's concentration risk will merely be hidden by a larger technology gain.

### Tonight's US Setup

**Base case:** Brent remains below USD 102 and the 10-year yield holds below 4.95%. US stocks open higher with technology and semiconductors leading, but some profit-taking emerges before the summit.

**Bull case:** The US-China AI dialogue and trade truce receive executable details, oil falls below USD 100 and breadth expands into small caps, industrials and consumer discretionary shares. Risk premia decline further for Taiwan and Malaysia's technology supply chains.

**Bear case:** Oil rebounds sharply during US trading, the 10-year yield returns to 5%, or Chinese officials contradict the positive negotiation narrative. A loss of semiconductor leadership and negative breadth after a higher open would turn crowded positioning into downside pressure.

### Market Impact

Lower oil and yields directly improve valuations for large technology, semiconductors and long-duration assets while easing cost pressure on airlines, transport and consumers. Energy shares lose relative support, and banks split between lower yields and better risk appetite. Taiwan receives continued chip-price confirmation; Malaysia's electrical and electronics and aviation sectors benefit, while upstream energy and some utilities lose oil support.

### Next Confirmation

Four conditions matter after the US cash open: the 10-year yield staying below 4.95%, Brent holding below USD 102, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and Russell 2000 rising together, and advancers clearly outnumbering decliners. All four would show that easier financial conditions have moved from futures prices into cash-market breadth.

The second test is bilateral confirmation of US-China and US-Iran diplomatic signals. One-sided positive language can reduce short-term risk premia, but only concrete provisions on the truce deadline, the AI dialogue or energy-transport security can change medium-term orders, credit and capital-spending assumptions.

## Global Markets and Research

### China-US Trade Talks

**Event and Facts:** US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng are meeting in New York, with trade, investment, artificial intelligence and the Middle East expected on the agenda before a leaders summit later this week. The current trade truce expires in November, so the economic value of the meeting depends on executable provisions covering an extension, purchases or export controls.

**Market Pricing:** S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures edged higher early Monday, while the dollar was little changed against the yen and yuan. Markets are assuming that friction will not escalate immediately, but they are not priced for a comprehensive settlement. **A bullish confirmation would require wider equity breadth, a stable yuan and a simultaneous decline in the risk premium on Asian semiconductor supply chains.**

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** Taiwan is most exposed through advanced chips, servers and electronics manufacturing, while Malaysia receives the second-order effect through electrical and electronics exports, data-center investment and multinational production allocation. The next test is whether officials publish specific language on the truce, technology controls or commodity purchases, followed by confirmation from the yuan, Asian chip shares and freight prices.

### Japan and the Yen

**Event and Facts:** Japan is observing a holiday on Monday, reducing cash-market liquidity as the yen remains near 157 per dollar after falling more than 2% last week. The currency did not strengthen after the Bank of Japan's rate increase, showing that the expected pace of future tightening, the US-Japan yield gap and policy dissent matter more than a single rate decision.

**Market Pricing:** A weaker yen supports Japanese exporters and dollar-asset returns, but extends imported inflation and pressure for further policy normalization. Holiday-thinned trading can magnify individual flows, so a quiet yen is not proof that funded positions are secure. Intervention risk increases if USD/JPY approaches 158.

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** The yen is a major Asian funding currency. A rapid rally would force leverage to contract and would first pressure high-valuation technology and foreign-flow-dependent markets; persistent weakness could instead lift Japanese yields and Asian term premia. Watch Japanese government bond yields, banks versus exporters and whether USD/JPY holds its level after normal liquidity returns.

### Middle East Energy and Shipping

**Event and Facts:** Brent remains near USD 104 a barrel. Prices have eased from last week's highs, but the supply-risk premium has not disappeared. Oil and liquefied-natural-gas shipments through the Strait of Hormuz reached a six-month high over the past two weeks, while the main transit lanes remained open, indicating that physical flows have not faced a comprehensive interruption.

**Market Pricing:** Oil holding above USD 100 reflects two conditions at once: shipping continues to function, yet Middle East supply remains vulnerable. **The premium has shifted from imminent disruption to a persistent insurance cost, so lower oil prices do not mean that energy and inflation risk has cleared.**

**Transmission and Next Confirmation:** Taiwan absorbs the effect through imported energy, transport and manufacturing costs. Malaysia is split between upstream oil and gas plus fiscal benefits on one side, and airlines, utilities and data centers on the other. The United States receives the shock through inflation expectations and long yields. Watch Brent around USD 100 and USD 105, tanker rates, transit volumes and whether oil again rises together with the 10-year Treasury yield.

## Cross-Market Transmission

The first chain runs from stronger US semiconductors to gains in TSMC ADRs and NVIDIA, supporting Taiwan's large electronics weights. A 5% Treasury yield and negative US breadth, however, concentrate capital in leaders that can demonstrate cash flow rather than lifting every artificial-intelligence theme.

The second chain runs through Taiwan positioning: heavy foreign cash buying, limited futures covering and higher domestic margin financing. Persistent breadth would indicate an institution-led trend. A higher open followed by weaker breadth would shift the risk from the index into leveraged, high-volatility smaller stocks.

The third chain links US-China negotiations and the yen to Asia's risk premium, while oil determines inflation and long yields. Taiwan receives the shock mainly through technology orders and foreign flows. Malaysia is exposed simultaneously through electronics exports, energy revenue, the ringgit and government-bond yields, creating different sector winners and losers from the same global event.

## Top Five Market Risks

1. Taiwan opens higher but the Taipei Exchange, advancing shares and turnover breadth fade quickly.
2. Foreign cash buying fails to continue, futures shorts rebuild and margin balances rise again.
3. The US 10-year yield holds decisively above 5%, compressing long-duration and high-multiple valuations.
4. US-China talks produce no concrete outcome, reviving risk around technology controls and the November truce deadline.
5. Brent returns above USD 105, lifting energy costs, inflation expectations and long yields together.

## What the Market May Be Missing

**The central question for Taiwan is no longer whether it can open higher, but whether foreign cash, futures hedges and domestic margin financing can form a durable combination.** Foreign investors bought TWD 86.99 billion in cash equities, yet retained a 76,110-contract futures net short as margin financing rose TWD 4.64 billion. If foreign buying does not continue, the added leverage will expose fragile positions faster than the index itself.

Malaysia presents another underappreciated divergence: its benchmark fell even as market breadth was positive and government bond yields declined. Large-cap pressure can coexist with domestic rotation into smaller technology and construction shares. The FBM KLCI alone understates that rotation, while breadth alone understates the valuation restraint from foreign flows and global yields.
