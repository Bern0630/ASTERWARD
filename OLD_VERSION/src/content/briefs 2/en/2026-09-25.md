Malaysian large caps finished nearly flat while smaller companies and technology turnover remained resilient, and US futures edged higher. That supports an initial US rebound attempt, but it does not remove the valuation pressure created by a 10-year Treasury yield still above 5%.

**Secondary signal 1:** The FBM KLCI slipped only 0.04%, with decliners narrowly ahead of advancers, while the Mid 70 and ACE indexes rose and technology turnover stayed active. Asia is showing selective support rather than broad risk aversion.

**Secondary signal 2:** US equity futures gained roughly 0.1% to 0.3% as oil eased from its highs, but the 10-year Treasury yield remained near 5.16% to 5.19%. Technology relative strength and post-open breadth must confirm the quality of any rebound.

## Evening Market Outlook

### Taiwan Market-Closed State

The Taiwan Stock Exchange was closed on September 25 for the Mid-Autumn Festival and will also be closed on September 28 for Teachers' Day. The next trading session is September 29. There are therefore no new Taiwan index, breadth, institutional cash-flow, foreign index-futures, options, margin, short-selling, or securities-lending data. This report does not present September 24 figures as current-session data.

The previous valid state remains useful context: the Taiwan Weighted Index closed at 48,024.60 on September 24, foreign investors sold a net TWD 32.965 billion in cash equities, and their Taiwan index-futures net short stood at 77,031 contracts. **What this means for the target market: the US session lacks a fresh Taiwan cash-market check on chip demand, so the prior defensive positioning is background rather than proof that Asian risk has improved.**

### Malaysia Closing Signal

The FBM KLCI lost 0.69 point, or 0.04%, to 1,671.62 after trading between 1,666.99 and 1,675.47. Decliners led advancers 590 to 575, while turnover expanded to 4.07 billion shares worth MYR 2.69 billion. Large-cap direction was mixed: Maybank and Tenaga Nasional rose, while CIMB and the plantation and industrial-products groups weakened; the energy index edged higher. Meanwhile, the Mid 70 gained 90.10 points, the ACE Index added 95.53 points, and technology turnover reached 1.17 billion shares, showing that investors did not broadly abandon risk assets.

Bernama separately reported that the ringgit closed higher against the US dollar, but a verifiable official closing rate was not obtained, so no exchange-rate level is stated. **What this means for the target market: Malaysia signals selective growth support under high rates, which can help a US technology rebound attempt but does not show that financial conditions have eased or breadth will expand broadly.**

### US Pre-Market Conditions

Major US exchanges are open today. Ahead of the session, S&P 500 and Dow futures were up about 0.1%, while Nasdaq 100 futures gained roughly 0.2% to 0.3%, leaving technology slightly ahead. WTI eased to about USD 92 per barrel and Brent to around USD 105, providing limited relief from the prior day's energy-inflation pressure. Bonds remain the binding constraint: the 10-year Treasury yield was still near 5.16% to 5.19% and the 30-year near 5.47%, showing that inflation, fiscal supply, and a higher policy-rate path remain priced.

The VIX ended the previous session at 15.67, up from 15.18. Because the US cash market has not opened, today's VIX close and market breadth are not yet observable. A verifiable official pre-market US Dollar Index level was also unavailable, so no number is supplied. **Lower oil can improve sentiment, but unless long yields decline, equity gains are more likely to represent discount-driven position rebuilding than a change in the valuation regime.**

### US Session Outlook

**The baseline is a modest rebound led by technology but limited in breadth.** Taiwan's closure removes a new chip-supply-chain signal, while Malaysia's technology turnover and small-cap gains provide a mild positive input. With Nasdaq futures leading, investors may first rebuild positions in mega-cap technology and semiconductors. Confirmation requires the 10-year yield below 5.15%, VIX staying below 16, Nasdaq continuing to outperform the S&P 500, and advancers exceeding decliners. At least three of those four conditions should hold before treating the move as broader than a large-cap rebound.

The downside path begins if the 10-year yield moves back above 5.20% while Brent returns above USD 106 or VIX rises above 17. Energy and rates would then compress technology valuations together, making a positive futures indication vulnerable to a fade after the open. A stronger upside path requires the yield below 5.10%, further oil weakness, and leadership expanding from semiconductors into software, consumer shares, and small caps. The sequence tonight is rates first, technology relative strength second, and breadth third; a green index alone does not confirm rebuilt risk appetite.

### Invalidation Conditions

The "rebound with limited breadth" thesis fails on the upside if the 10-year yield holds below 5.10%, VIX stays under 15.5, and advancers clearly exceed decliners; that would justify upgrading to broad risk recovery. It fails on the downside if the yield exceeds 5.20%, VIX rises above 17, and Nasdaq leadership reverses, shifting the outlook to a discount-rate-led decline.
