**Primary thesis:** All three markets benefited from lower oil and US long-term yields, but risk appetite had not broadened decisively. Taiwan paired strong cash buying with substantial futures hedging, Malaysia improved in cash-market quality while derivatives stayed cautious, and the US entered the pre-market with less valuation pressure but incomplete momentum confirmation.

**Secondary signal 1:** Taiwan's leadership rotated from TSMC alone toward MediaTek, panels, substrates and selected smaller electronics names, yet declining stocks still outnumbered advancers on the main board. Rotation was not the same as a broad bull market.

**Secondary signal 2:** Malaysia's rebound came with higher turnover and a stable ringgit, making it stronger than the previous session. Futures discount and lower open interest, however, looked more like risk reduction than newly established leveraged longs.

## Morning Market Outlook

### Previous US Session

US equities rebounded. The S&P 500 rose 1.49% to 7,764.70, the Nasdaq gained 2.26%, the Dow added 0.71%, and the Russell 2000 advanced only 0.52%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 4.3%, NVIDIA gained 2.3%, and TSMC ADRs increased 2.41% to USD 445.14. Capital returned first to large technology and semiconductors rather than lifting every stock evenly. Eight S&P 500 sectors finished higher and advancers outnumbered decliners by roughly 1.4 to one, while Nasdaq new lows still exceeded new highs. Breadth improved without fully confirming the rally.

Cross-asset conditions supported valuation repair. US 2-year, 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields closed at 4.76%, 4.96% and 5.29%, with the 10-year down from about 5.01% in the previous session. Brent fell to USD 100.34 per barrel and the dollar index was near 99.98. Lower oil and long yields reduced inflation and discount-rate pressure, but absolute rates remained high. Markets were pricing less pressure, not easy financial conditions.

### Previous Taiwan Session

The Taiex had gained 1.14% to 47,718.84 on turnover of TWD 820.22 billion, while the OTC index slipped 0.03%. The three major institutional groups bought a combined TWD 47.04 billion: foreign investors bought TWD 20.38 billion, investment trusts bought TWD 3.93 billion, and dealers bought TWD 22.73 billion. Foreign investors held a net short of 74,081 index-futures contracts, down 2,029 contracts from the prior day. The option volume put/call ratio was 125.10% and the open-interest ratio was 96.20%. Cash buying and futures short covering were constructive, while options retained downside protection.

Margin financing value increased by about TWD 9.00 billion to TWD 603.04 billion and financed shares rose by 53,300 lots; securities-lending short balances eased slightly. **Index heavyweights, institutional cash demand and less futures hedging supported the upside, but faster local leverage increased sensitivity near the high.** The test was not simply whether the index made another high, but whether the OTC market, advancing issues and turnover followed.

### Taiwan Session Outlook

The base case was a firm semiconductor-led open whose quality depended on breadth. TSMC ADRs, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and NVIDIA all rose, directly supporting TSMC, advanced process, packaging and server suppliers. If smaller electronics and the OTC market failed to strengthen, the move would be US semiconductor price transmission rather than a broad increase in Taiwan risk appetite. Continued foreign cash buying, a smaller futures net short and a stable Taiwan dollar were required for trend confirmation.

### US Pre-Market Outlook

The US pre-market bias was constructive but not suitable for chasing. As long as the 10-year yield stayed below 5% and Brent did not reclaim USD 102, technology and semiconductors could retain relative strength. A follow-through from the Russell 2000, financials and consumer discretionary stocks would be needed to upgrade valuation repair into a broader risk rally.

### Invalidation Conditions

The Taiwan call would fail if a higher open was followed by weaker OTC performance, quickly narrowing breadth and renewed foreign futures shorts. The US pre-market call would fail if the 10-year yield returned above 5%, oil rebounded and semiconductor leadership weakened together.

## Evening Market Outlook

The morning direction was not fully invalidated, but closing structure mattered more than the headline index move. Taiwan received support from foreign cash and selected electronics groups but did not retain its intraday high. Malaysia's rebound quality improved. The US still needed participation beyond semiconductors after the open.

### Taiwan Market

The Taiex closed at 47,800.17, up 81.33 points or 0.17%, on turnover of about TWD 1.079 trillion. It reached 48,601.53 intraday and closed at the day's low. The OTC index rose 0.63% to 415.17, while 330 main-board stocks advanced and 636 declined. TSMC fell 0.81% to TWD 2,460, MediaTek rose 3.39%, and panels and substrates strengthened on heavy volume. **The index retreated roughly 801 points from the high, showing heavier supply in large caps; a higher OTC close showed rotation rather than wholesale risk reduction.**

Foreign investors bought TWD 45.32 billion and the three major institutional groups bought TWD 60.81 billion, while investment trusts sold a modest TWD 0.37 billion. Foreign investors held 10,740 long and 86,308 short index-futures contracts, a net short of 75,568 contracts, up 1,487 from the previous day. The option volume put/call ratio was 101.40% and the open-interest ratio was 87.33%. Strong cash buying alongside more futures hedging implied selective equity exposure with index-tail protection.

Financed shares fell by about 85,100 lots to 9.245 million lots, but margin financing value increased by about TWD 1.82 billion to TWD 604.86 billion. Securities-lending short balances fell by roughly 72 million shares. Lower-priced leveraged positions declined while financing value shifted toward expensive leaders. Fundamentally, August export orders reached USD 102.96 billion, up 71.4% year on year, and seasonally adjusted unemployment was 3.32%. The demand lead indicator was strong, but it did not automatically translate into a broad current-quarter cash-flow upgrade.

The next session would qualify as healthy rotation if 47,800 held, TSMC stabilized, the OTC market retained relative strength and foreign futures shorts stopped increasing. A net short above 76,000 contracts, weaker foreign cash buying and another rise in financing value would make the intraday high look more like near-term supply.

### Malaysia Market

The FBM KLCI gained 16.56 points, or 0.99%, to 1,683.50. There were 678 advancers and 458 decliners; volume increased from 2.93 billion to 3.57 billion shares and value rose to MYR 3.15 billion. Banks and healthcare led while the energy index weakened with lower oil. **Price, breadth and turnover improved together, making this a higher-quality rebound than one driven only by index heavyweights.**

The ringgit moved from 4.0755/4.0800 to 4.0730/4.0775 against the US dollar and did not contradict the equity rebound. KLCI futures rose to 1,673 but remained 10.5 points below cash, while open interest fell from 51,310 to 48,706 contracts. That combination suggested short covering more than fresh leveraged longs. Bank Negara Malaysia reopened MYR 5 billion of seven-year government bonds at a 2.17 bid-to-cover ratio. Demand absorbed supply, but long-end rates above roughly 4% continued to constrain capital-intensive valuations.

Banks, healthcare and electronics retained rotation potential if the KLCI held 1,670-1,680, the ringgit stayed stable and turnover did not fade sharply. Wider futures discount without better foreign flows would keep the rebound in the category of risk recovery rather than a confirmed earnings-cycle upgrade for data centers and export electronics.

### US Pre-Market and Session Outlook

US equity futures were modestly higher, with Nasdaq futures leading. The US 10-year yield eased to about 4.93% and Brent moved near USD 98.29, reducing valuation and inflation pressure together. These conditions supported large technology, small caps and consumer discretionary stocks, but limited futures gains after the prior session's technology rally showed that the market still needed price confirmation.

The base case was high-level consolidation in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq with semiconductors retaining relative strength. Continued gains in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index accompanied by stronger Russell 2000, financial and consumer discretionary performance would confirm broader risk appetite. If only a few artificial-intelligence leaders rose, positioning would remain concentrated. A fixed three-scenario framework was unnecessary because the important dividing lines were already the 5% US 10-year yield and USD 100 Brent.

### Invalidation Conditions

Lower oil and long yields were the common constructive condition across all three markets. The outlook would fail together if Brent reclaimed USD 100, the 10-year yield returned above 5%, and semiconductors weakened. If cross-asset conditions remained supportive but US breadth contracted, the breadth call would be downgraded without invalidating relative strength in large technology.
