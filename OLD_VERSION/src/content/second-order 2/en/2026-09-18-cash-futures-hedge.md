## Research Thesis

When foreign investors buy cash equities aggressively while retaining a large futures hedge, the question is not which market is right. The analytical task is to trace how the combination changes basis, single-stock volatility, domestic leverage and the durability of the advance. The first-order result is a higher index; the second-order effects determine who ultimately carries the risk.

## Evidence and Market Pricing

Taiwan's three major institutional groups bought a combined NT$116.587 billion on September 18, including NT$86.994 billion from foreign investors. The TAIEX gained 892.75 points, or 1.93%, and closed at the session high of 47,180.75. Listed-market turnover returned to NT$1.075 trillion and the OTC index gained 3.64%. The first-order interpretation is straightforward: foreign re-entry and a US semiconductor rebound strengthened price, volume and breadth together.

Futures did not provide the same degree of directional confirmation. The foreign index-futures net short declined from 78,674 to 76,110 contracts, a reduction of only 2,564. **Cash buying was much stronger than futures short covering, indicating that new demand targeted stocks and cash exposure rather than a broad increase in index beta.** This is where the second-order effects begin.

## Transmission Mechanism

Cash demand first strengthens price and breadth, while futures hedging changes index beta and basis, which then affects arbitrage, market making and domestic momentum demand. If cash buying broadens and the futures net short declines, protection can evolve into directional risk-taking. If foreign cash demand fades while margin financing takes over, risk migrates from institutional hedges to domestic leverage.

## Second-Order Effect One: Basis and Hedging Cost

When large cash buying lifts the underlying market while futures retain substantial hedging demand, the relationship between futures and spot affects arbitrageurs and market makers. If futures trade at a discount, arbitrage capital can buy futures and sell cash, partly offsetting the rally. If futures trade at a premium, market-making and hedging flows can add to heavyweight demand. The direction requires the observed basis, dividends, funding cost and expiry structure; net shorts alone are not sufficient.

Quarterly derivatives expiration adds complexity. Rolls, settlement and option exercise can amplify turnover without representing a new fundamental view. Friday's index-options volume put/call ratio was 99.35% and the open-interest ratio was 76.32%. Trading was balanced between puts and calls while the stock of protection remained relatively low, suggesting that immediate volatility could be driven more by futures and cash adjustments.

## Second-Order Effect Two: Volatility Moves From the Index to Stocks

A large index hedge can reduce a portfolio's sensitivity to broad declines and make institutions more willing to hold high-conviction stocks. Headline index volatility may remain controlled while relative volatility between companies rises. TSMC, MediaTek, ASE and many AI suppliers gained, Hon Hai was flat, and financials declined. Stock-selection returns exceeded uniform sector movement.

When company news is positive, the hedge allows capital to buy beneficiaries without buying the whole market. When news turns negative, institutions may first sell liquid heavyweights while covering part of their futures shorts. **A large futures net short does not only suppress the index; it can also accelerate rotation, producing faster separation between winners and losers beneath a strong headline market.**

## Second-Order Effect Three: Domestic Leverage Can Take Over

Foreign cash demand improves momentum and can attract domestic margin financing, short-term traders and passive flows. If official margin balances increase sharply next session while foreign cash buying fades, the marginal buyer may shift from institutional capital to short-duration leverage. That transition can look identical in the index but changes the source of selling during a reversal.

Final official September 18 margin, short-sale and securities-lending balances were not yet available, so September 17 data should not be substituted. The second-order assessment must wait for the official balance and combine it with foreign cash, futures, options and breadth. Rising margin with strong OTC participation and breadth has market support. Rising margin with narrowing breadth is considerably more fragile.

## Second-Order Effect Four: Concentration of Cash Buying

A large institutional purchase value does not distribute money equally across shares. Index products, heavyweights, arbitrage and specific sector re-entry may account for most of the amount. Friday's 759 listed advancers and 3.64% OTC gain confirmed genuine breadth, but weaker financials and flat Hon Hai still showed that capital was selective.

Concentration determines durability. If foreign buying expands from TSMC into packaging, design, servers, networking, power and smaller suppliers, the market can progress from short covering to an earnings-led advance. If buying remains concentrated in a few large weights, any increase in futures hedging will make the index unusually sensitive to selling in those names.

## Effects on Market Participants

Long-horizon investors should treat large cash buying as better liquidity, not as the removal of valuation risk. The US ten-year yield remains near 5% and oil remains above $100. Corporate cash flow and returns on capital still determine medium-term outcomes.

Short-term traders face higher overnight correlation risk. Taiwan has already priced the US semiconductor rebound. If Friday's US session reverses on industrial data, yields or oil, Monday's open could reflect ADR repricing and futures protection together. High turnover does not mean low gap risk.

Companies and suppliers may receive better equity-financing conditions from higher share prices, but debt costs have not fallen by the same amount. Any accelerated expansion still needs to prove utilization, margins and payback rather than treating an equity rebound as a broad reduction in the cost of capital.

## Bull Case

The constructive path requires three to five sessions of foreign cash buying, a futures net short below 70,000 contracts, a higher options open-interest put/call ratio and growth in margin financing below growth in turnover and market value. Cash demand would be replacing hedge-dominated positioning, the basis would remain orderly, and market breadth could persist.

Stronger confirmation would come from simultaneous gains in TSMC ADR, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and Taiwan's OTC market while the US ten-year yield stays below 5%. The market would then move from a policy-relief rally to joint support from earnings and capital flows.

## Bear Case

The adverse path is simultaneous foreign cash selling, a larger futures net short, rapidly rising margin financing and weaker OTC breadth. Marginal demand would have shifted from institutions to domestic leverage while index protection increased. Once prices fall below short-term cost bases, margin reduction, trend-following futures and passive weight adjustments can reinforce one another.

Another route is a joint break higher in US two-year and ten-year yields. Foreign investors need not sell all Taiwan cash equities immediately. They can add futures shorts, reduce individual positions or raise currency hedges. Liquidity would contract first in smaller and high-multiple companies before reaching the index.

## Leading Indicators

1. Whether three-day cumulative foreign cash flow and changes in the futures net short align.
2. The front-month futures basis, roll spread and rebuilding of open interest after expiry.
3. Divergence between options volume and open-interest put/call ratios.
4. Listed and OTC advancers, turnover and new highs.
5. Whether margin balances grow faster than market value and turnover.
6. Whether securities lending shifts from index protection into single-stock shorting.
7. The joint direction of US yields, the dollar, oil and TSMC ADR.

## Falsification

If foreign futures shorts decline sharply, cash buying persists and domestic margin stays stable, the second-order risk described here would be invalidated by a more complete risk-on regime. If the cash purchase was merely index rebalancing or one-time arbitrage and reverses next session, the durable-inflow assumption would also fail and the move would need to be treated as event volatility.

## Next Confirmation

The next session should first test whether foreign cash buying continues and whether the futures net short falls below 70,000 contracts. Official margin, short-sale and securities-lending changes come next. Rising prices, durable breadth, controlled leverage and lower hedging would make the second-order structure constructive. Stalled prices, rising margin and larger futures shorts would shift risk from visible volatility into positioning fragility.
